Cadence Manual

The SHLabs hybrid performance mixer — four channels of decks, live inputs, plugins and automation, locked to one clock.

Welcome

Cadence is a performance mixer built around one idea: everything follows one clock. Four channels each play either a live input (hardware, synths, a eurorack mix) or an internal deck (a track from your collection). Decks beat-match to the master tempo, launches and loops land on the grid, plugins follow the host clock, and partner instruments can even follow the key of the playing track — so backing tracks, loops, hardware and VST instruments all stay in time and in tune while you mix.

  • Perform — the mixer: four strips, master section, track overviews.
  • Collection — your library: scanning, search, playlists, key-compatible browsing.
  • Composer — a timeline: lay out a set in advance with automation envelopes.
  • Sampler — a sample editor: snip sounds out of tracks, render strip re-edits.

Quick start

  • 1. Add music. Go to COLLECTION, click Add Files (or Import XML for a rekordbox collection).
  • 2. Scan. Select tracks and press Scan. This detects BPM, the beatgrid with its bar downbeat, the musical key, and the loudness (for auto-gain). Several tracks scan in parallel.
  • 3. Load. Use the LOAD 1–4 buttons (they glow with the live level of each channel so you don't load over a playing deck), or right-click → Load playing (fader down) to audition.
  • 4. Play. Back in PERFORM, press a deck's PLAY (or keys Q W E R). The button blinks while armed and fires on the next quantize point. A deck starts the master clock itself, so there is nothing to press first — the master PLAY lives in COMPOSER, for running the timeline.
  • 5. Mix. SYNC keeps decks locked to the master tempo (with key-lock, so pitch doesn't bend). Ride the faders and EQs, drop loops, and watch the track overview for the red end-of-track warning.
Tip: press H any time (or Keyboard shortcuts… in SETTINGS) for the shortcut overlay — it also shows the QR codes for the remote control and this manual. Any key or a click closes it.

The guided tour

The tour shows Cadence one part at a time. The window dims, the part lights up, and a card beside it says what it does. Start it from SETTINGS → HELP → Guided tour…, or with TAKE THE TOUR on the welcome card a new install opens with. Pick a chapter (Perform, Collection, Composer, Sampler, Mapping and macros) or the whole tour.

  • NEXT and BACK, or the arrow keys, move through the steps.
  • AUTO, or Space, plays the tour by itself and gives each step time to read. A thin line along the card shows the time. PAUSE stops it.
  • CLOSE or Esc ends the tour. Cadence goes back to the view you were in, with your panels as you left them.

The tour only shows: while it runs, the app under it takes no clicks or keys. A step about a deck's controls needs a track on that deck; with none, it shows the whole deck and says so.

The interface

Mode tabs (top left)

PERFORM COMPOSER COLLECTION SAMPLER switch the whole surface (keys 1–4, left to right). The transport bar stays on top in every mode.

Transport bar

PLAY starts the master clock (and sends MIDI Start). The BPM slider (60–180) eases to a new tempo gradually instead of snapping. T is tap-tempo, M the metronome (right-click it to route the click to master and/or headphones and set its level). Quantize — the one grid for everything — lives in SETTINGS; see Transport & quantize. When Cadence follows a MIDI clock, the tempo's tag reads MIDI instead of BPM; see Following a MIDI clock.

Top bar buttons

Left to right: BROWSE (dock the collection into PERFORM, COMPOSER or SAMPLER, also C), ASSIST (auto-mixing — right-click for its options, among them Hold: taken while a mix is running it freezes that transition — faders, EQ, filters and stem moves together — where it stands, and releasing it carries on from exactly that point) and REC. Then, after a gap, the configuration group: FX (delay & reverb), COMPRESSOR, OSC, MAP (MIDI, macro and OSC mapping — PERFORM and COMPOSER) and SETTINGS. Beside them sit the engine readout (buffer · output latency · DSP load — click it for engine options) and the CADENCE wordmark (click for About & licenses).

The top bar stays deliberately short: the plugin manager, the MIDI mapping list, UI zoom, the waveform colour choice and the shortcut overlay all live inside SETTINGS.

Channel strips

Each of the four strips is one channel. The beat dots in its header show the deck's bar phase against the master grid, and the readout beside them counts the track: elapsed / −remaining while it plays, LEN while it is parked. On a narrow deck column that line has to share the row with the beat dots, the activity marks and the source and record chips, so the clock says less rather than saying it smaller: it drops to the remaining time alone (−4:52), then to whole minutes or seconds (−4m, −52s), and the deck title drops from CH 4 to 4. Nothing is ever squashed or cut off — a coarse rung is always floored, so “−4m” means at least four minutes are left.

The MIDI and OSC marks

Two marks in a deck's header show what the channel sends. The MIDI mark is a round socket with three pins: it lights while the channel plays notes, brighter for louder notes. The OSC mark is a dot with two arcs: it lights while the channel sends values on the module bus, brighter for higher values. A mark is there only while its signal flows. Hover a mark to read what it is and where its signal comes from. The notes can come from the channel's MIDI clip, its plugins, a controller, the Composer's MIDI lane or the loaded track's own MIDI layer; the values come from the Composer's OSC envelope or the track's own OSC layer. When the track's own layer lights a mark, the tooltip names the track and says where to edit or remove the layer (see Collection). A Composer lane header shows the same two marks in the same order, MIDI first, while the lane holds MIDI clips or an OSC envelope.

Player panel

  • Source — toggles between INPUT (the audio interface input for this channel) and PLAYER (the internal deck).
  • Clip name — click to load a file; right-click to eject the track (your cue/loop edits are saved back to the collection first). You can also drag an audio file straight onto the strip.
  • PLAY — quantized launch: blinks while armed, starts exactly on the grid. Press again to stop (also quantized). De-clicked fades on start/stop.
  • LOOP — whether the clip loops at its end or plays one-shot.
  • SYNC — locks the deck's beatgrid to the master clock (beat-matched, phase-snapped). On by default.
  • →BPM — glides the master tempo gradually to this track's original BPM (ease the whole set back to native speed, unnoticed).
  • KEY — key-lock and key shifting; see Key-lock.
  • FX chip — shows the plugin chain ("EQ > Comp"); click it for the plugin menu. See Plugins.

Perform panel

  • Hot cues — one pad per saved cue: click to jump there (quantized), shift-click to delete. Right-click a pad to name it (type one, or take one of the eight quick names — Intro, Verse, Build, Drop, Break, Chorus, Outro, Mix), to give it a colour from the section palette, or to delete it. The name and the colour are stored with the track and travel with it; a colour you pick by hand keeps it when other cues are deleted and the set renumbers. A pad shows the name where the deck is wide enough for the whole word and the cue’s NUMBER where it is not — it never squashes one — and the overview strip under the wave carries the same colour, the same number and the name beside the flag.
  • < > — beat-jump by the quantize size (shift-click = 4×).
  • SLIP, beside the jumps. Lit, the track keeps its own time under loops, held hot cues and held jumps, and the deck comes back to it when you let go. Right-click a hot cue pad for pitch play: that cue on eight pads at eight keys. See Slip & pitch play.
  • /2 LOOP 4 x2 — the auto-loop: loop N beats from the current beat; halve/double the length live. The button pulses while looping.
  • 1/4 1/2 1 2 — loop rolls: hold to stutter, release to snap back in time. See Loops & rolls.

Sound shaping

Three-band EQ (HIGH/MID/LOW, full kill at the bottom), a dual-slope FILTER (low-pass left of centre, high-pass right), and DLY/VERB sends into the built-in tempo delay and reverb (their character is set in the FX panel). The FX+/FX− VOL+/VOL− FLT+/FLT− chips run gradual automation ramps over bars — hands-free build-ups and fade-outs.

Fader, trim and routing

The channel fader tops out at 0 dB — headroom lives on the master only. Under the hood every scanned track also carries an auto-gain trim toward −14 LUFS, so decks come up at comparable loudness. CUE sends the channel pre-fader to your headphones; ON assigns it to the master bus (quantized mute/unmute).

With a hardware mixer, each deck can leave on its own output pair instead. See External mixer.

Centre console & MACROS

The mixer console sits between the deck columns. Its header cycles through three sizes on each click, so you can trade console for deck space mid-set:

  • MIXER — the full channel strips.
  • MIXER - LEVELS — bare level strips: meter, fader and PLUGINS only.
  • MIXER - HIDDEN — no console at all; the decks take the whole width.

The header row itself always stays, so the console can never get lost. Drag either edge to trade width with the decks — the full and compact sizes remember their own widths.

STEMS rows — click the STEMS line under a strip’s header to show each deck’s four stem levels, DRUMS, BASS, OTHER and VOCALS, above its EQ. One click opens or closes them on that channel only, like the EQ, and Cadence remembers each channel’s choice. They start closed. Each bar is that stem’s own level, the same one the deck’s chip drag and a mapped knob set: full is 0 dB, half-way is −12 dB, empty is off. Drag a bar to set it; double-click it for 0 dB. Click a stem’s name to mute it on the next bar, exactly as its chip on the deck does (shift-click for instant). Right-click the name for the stem's menu, the one a long press on its chip opens: Echo out, the held Roll lengths and its sends. A small square after the name shows the state: filled means audible, hollow means muted, and it blinks while a mute waits for its bar. A muted stem’s name and bar go dim. When a deck has no stems to play — not separated yet, still separating, or held by a warped grid — the rows stay where they are, greyed, and hovering one says why. In MIDI MAP mode, click a stem’s bar to learn a knob for that stem’s level. The rows are hidden in the MIXER - LEVELS size. Assist’s and the Composer’s stem moves ride on top of these levels and never reset them, and the bar shows them: while one holds a stem below your level, the bar is lit up to what the stem plays and dim from there up to your level. Letters above CUE and MAIN show which Composer envelopes drive the channel: V volume, F filter, D delay, R reverb and S stems.

MACROS is its own collapsible band at the bottom of the console (click the header to open or close it): eight slots in two columns, M1–M4 down the left, M5–M8 down the right, each captioned with its name and, when it drives more than one thing, its target count; a LEARN chip and a page navigator sit in the row above. Each slot draws the face of its kind: a bar (fader or knob), a button, a momentary pad (hold) or a row of radio stops. It hides with the console. See Master section.

Master section & macros

  • Master fader — the only fader allowed above 0 dB. Stereo meters beside it.
  • LIMIT — burns red while the brick-wall safety limiter is actually reducing gain. If you see it often, pull the master down.
  • COMPRESSOR — the master glue compressor: threshold, ratio, soft knee, attack/release, makeup, and a sidechain high-pass so the kick stops pumping the mix. Live gain-reduction meter with 3/6/12 dB marks.
  • MACROS M1–M8 — your own control panel. A macro drives up to eight targets at once: any Cadence control (a channel's volume, EQ, filter, delay and reverb sends, stem levels and sends, the master, cue and phones, the built-in delay and reverb character, the master compressor, each deck's key from -6 to +6 semitones, and every button, roll and hold pad) or any parameter of any loaded plugin. Each target has its own range and invert, so one macro can open a filter while it closes a send. Learn: press LEARN (or right-click a macro → Learn targets, or MAP → MACRO); the window dims and every control a macro can drive is outlined, and the macro's editor sits at the right. Click a control to add it, right-click to remove it, click another macro (or the arrows in the banner) to arm that one, move a control that is mapped to your controller, or move a knob in a plugin window: each adds it. Pick the kind and drag the ranges in the editor while you go. DONE or Esc ends it. A macro shows the name you give it, else the name of its first target, else M and its number; when its targets go, so does that name. A control with nothing on screen to click (a deck's key, the delay and reverb character, the compressor) is in the macro's right-click menu under Add Cadence control, grouped by channel. Edit: right-click a macro → Edit targets opens the editor: rename, pick the kind, drag each target's range ends, INV, remove, CLEAR. Kinds: there are three. A slider moves continuously. An on/off switches between two states. A radio gives two to eight exclusive steps. What a kind does to a target depends on the target. On a fader, a knob or a plugin parameter, a slider and a radio set it through its range, and an on/off sets it to the top when on and the bottom when off. A press of an on/off first reads where the control sits, so a fader someone moved by hand still flips to the other end. INV swaps the ends. On an on/off control such as CUE, SYNC or LOOP, a slider switches it on in the upper half of its range and off in the lower half (the range moves that point, so 60–100 % switches at 80 %; INV swaps the halves), and an on/off sets it on or off. A one-shot control such as a beat jump or a hot cue fires when a slider rises past the middle or when an on/off turns on. Momentary is a binding rather than a kind: bind a controller pad to a macro's Hold target (in the mapping list) and the macro is on while the pad is held, whatever its kind. A set saved with the older kinds opens with the nearest of the three: a knob becomes a slider, a button or a hold becomes an on/off. A radio's stops are categories: on an on/off control, the editor lists which stops switch it on. In the editor, a line under each target says what the kind does to it, in amber when the pairing is doubtful: a range of one value, or a control another macro also drives (the macro moved last wins). A macro cannot drive another macro, a jog wheel or a button that only opens a window; those are not outlined while you learn. Each slot is MIDI- and OSC-learnable (the bar as Macro N, its press and hold as their own targets), so a hardware knob or pad drives the whole set of targets. The plugin editor's TO MACRO sends the last-touched parameter to a macro: to a new one, of the kind you pick, or to one you already have. A Composer lane's own envelope keeps its control while the set plays, and a macro on a deck's filter or volume pauses while Assist mixes that deck; the macro shows why. Assignments persist.
  • CUE/MIX & PHONES — headphone blend between the cue bus and the master, and the headphone level.

External mixer

Use this mode when a hardware mixer does the mixing, for example a club mixer with its own USB audio interface. Each deck leaves Cadence on its own output pair, and the mixer does the rest.

Set it up. In SETTINGS → Audio, set Mixing to External mixer. The row after it shows Deck outputs: the layouts your device can hold, such as 1-2, 3-4, 5-6, 7-8. Pick the one that matches your mixer’s channels, or use Custom per deck to put any deck on any pair. Two decks never share a pair: when you move a deck onto a pair another deck has, the two swap. Cadence opens the outputs the layout needs. It fades out first and fades back in, so nothing clicks. The mode and the layout are saved, and Cadence starts in them next time.

What each deck sends. The deck’s source, its trim, its stems, its whole plugin chain (the slots before and after the fader, in order) and its latency compensation, so every deck output stays aligned to the sample. It leaves before Cadence’s EQ, filter, delay, channel limiter and fader. Your mixer does those. There is no limiter on a deck output: the trim still stops a track from going past full scale, and a plugin that pushes it further clips at the interface, as it would on any hardware input.

What switches off, and why. The master, the cue, and the delay and reverb buses produce nothing in this mode. Hover any greyed control to read the reason.

  • Strips: EQ, FILTER, DELAY, REVERB, the fader, MAIN and CUE grey out in place. The meter stays live and shows what leaves on the deck’s pair.
  • Master section: OUT, COMPRESSOR, FX, CUE/MIX and PHONES grey out. Your mixer has its own.
  • REC records Cadence’s internal mix, so it is off. Record the mixer’s output instead. You cannot switch to an external mixer while a recording runs: stop it first.
  • Metronome: the click has no bus to play on. A take’s count-in still counts on the record chip, but you do not hear the click. Takes themselves record as before.
  • Sampler: the preview has no bus either, so PLAY, CUE, MAIN and VOL grey out there.
  • Stems: mutes and stem levels still work: they change the deck’s own sound. Per-stem DLY and VERB sends feed Cadence’s delay and reverb, so they are held, and the chips show a hollow send mark. Echo out is greyed for the same reason; the stem roll works.
  • Assist still picks the next track. It loads it onto a free deck, beat- and key-matched, and waits: you start it and mix it on the mixer. When the deck Assist follows stops, whatever still plays is the new live song, and Assist picks again. It never starts a deck and never moves a fader, an EQ, a stem or an effect, so Mix now, Hold, Undo last mix, the mode presets, the mixing features and Manual drop are greyed in its menu.
  • Phone remote: each deck’s fader, the FADE buttons and MIX NOW are gone from the page, and the server refuses them too. Play, loop, seek and the Next Up queue still work.
  • OSC: each deck’s level and bands follow its own output. The master bands read silence, because Cadence has no master to measure.

The system check. With an external mixer, the Audio device row needs one output pair for each deck you show, and turns red when the device has too few. The Deck outputs row turns red when a deck you show has no pair on the device, and it has the layout combo in the row. The fix is one of three: show fewer decks, pick another layout, or switch Mixing back to Internal.

Switching back. Set Mixing to Internal. Cadence fades out, switches and fades back in, and the EQ, filter and faders come back where you left them.

TRACKS band

Across the top of PERFORM, TRACKS shows the full-length waveform of every loaded deck side by side — always frequency-coloured (bass warm, mids green, highs blue), so you read each track's shape and energy at a glance while deciding what comes next.

Only decks with something in them get a lane (a track, a radio stream, or a MIDI instrument), so the band is as tall as it has things to show: it grows as you load and shrinks as you eject, and with every deck empty it steps aside to leave just its header.

Each lane starts with its channel, CH 1 to CH 4, and then the name of the track on it, so you can see which lane is which deck.

  • Click a lane to seek that deck (quantized). Clicking a stopped deck starts it from that point.
  • Drag the divider below the band to make the lanes taller or shorter.
  • Click the header to collapse the band to a single row and give the height back to the decks.

Cues, loops and section editing stay in the deck overviews — this band is for seeing and seeking.

PADS band

Click PADS, above WAVEFORM, to open the Sampler’s eight pads in PERFORM: short loops, hits and clips to play over the mix. They are the same pads as in the Sampler, so a pad you make there is here, and the other way round. The header counts the pads that hold something (“PADS 3/8”), and PERFORM remembers whether the band is open.

  • Play: press a pad, or 1–8 while the band is open. A pad that waits for its beat pulses on the beat.
  • Fill a pad: drop a track or clip on it from the Collection, or right-click a track in the Collection and choose Put on a pad. Right-click a pad for Take CH 1’s loop: the loop set on a deck becomes a loop pad that starts on the bar. Put the selected track here takes the track selected in the Collection.
  • In time: a pad whose tempo is known (from its track’s grid) plays at the master’s tempo, so a loop stays in time with the mix. The pitch moves with the tempo, like a deck without key lock. Turn it off per pad with Follow the master tempo; the pad then plays at its own speed. The pad shows its own tempo.
  • Settings: the right-click menu sets how the pad plays (once, loop, while held), when it starts (at once, on the next 1/16 or 1/8 note, beat or bar) and where it goes. Edit in the Sampler opens the pad’s region there.
  • Through a channel: Out > CH 1–CH 4 plays a pad through that channel, as if its deck played it: the channel’s plugins, EQ, filter, sends, fader and crossfader all shape it, and its fader closes it. The pad face shows CH2. Main and Headphones skip the channels.
  • Level: right-click a pad for Level (-12 to +6 dB), or hold Alt and drag the pad up or down; the face shows the level while you drag. The level is saved with the pad.
  • Length and speed: while the band is open, its header row carries LEN and SPEED chips for the selected pad (the one you last clicked or played from the keys, outlined). LEN repeats only the first 1/8 of a beat up to 8 beats of a loop or gate pad, in time with the master; ALL plays all of it, and picks up where the pad would have been. A held gate pad with a LEN keeps repeating while held, so 1/8 or 1/4 makes a roll. SPEED plays the pad at half or double time; the pitch moves an octave with it. Both are in the right-click menu too, and in the Sampler’s pad menu.
  • Steps: STEPS turns the band into a step row: the eight pads as small buttons on the left, the selected pad’s name, and its sixteen steps, one bar of sixteenths. Click a step to set or clear it, or drag across steps to paint them. RUN plays every pad’s steps while the master runs; it pulses on the beat. A step plays a one-shot from its start; steps in a row hold a loop or gate pad, which stops on the first empty step. A pad with steps shows them as ticks along its top edge in the PADS view.
  • Record steps: REC (it turns RUN on) writes the pads you play onto the nearest sixteenth. A hit just before a step plays on that step; one just after plays at once. Hold a loop or gate pad for as long as it should play: every step it is held across is written. Clear its steps in a pad’s menu empties its row.
  • Mapping: each pad is a MAP target, from this band, its step row or the Sampler’s. RUN, REC, every LEN chip and the three speeds are targets too.

Track overviews & WAVEFORM

Each deck carries its own overview of the loaded track:

  • Click anywhere to seek there (quantized) and play. Each lane is tagged CH n with a live level meter so strips and lanes stay linked.
  • Sections from the section editor show as coloured, labelled bands; hot cues as numbered flags in each cue’s own colour with its name beside them; the loop region in azure with draggable IN/OUT handles.
  • End-of-track warning — the remaining tail pulses red inside 30 seconds and shows a countdown inside 15 (quiet while a loop is engaged).
  • Waveform colour — FREQ in the PERFORM top bar, or in SETTINGS: Song sections (the section colours speak) or Frequency tint (bass warm, mids green, highs blue). The Composer has its own FREQ; it is the same switch, so a press in either view changes both. It can be mapped to MIDI (Waveform Tint (FREQ)).
  • A ghost playhead appears during a loop roll — the real position running underneath the stutter.
  • A nudge ghost appears when you slide a recorded take (the NUDGE line in the WAVEFORM band, or SNAP): a dashed line where the material used to sit, a band showing how far it moved, and the amount. While it reads applies at loop the engine has not taken the change yet — a nudge lands at the next loop wrap, never mid-lap — and the ghost fades as that boundary takes it.

WAVEFORM

The WAVEFORM band at the very bottom is a collapsible section header — click it to open or close. It scrolls the played audio of every deck against the master grid, with a zoom control, so you can see at a glance whether two decks' transients line up. Display only: click-seeking and loop-dragging live in the deck overviews above.

Down its right-hand side is a diagnostics column, one row per lane and on that lane's own line, so a reading and the wave it belongs to are always side by side. Each row shows the deck's CH number, a bar whose length is RMS (how loud the deck is) with a tick on it at peak (how close to clipping — it turns amber, then red), both of those repeated as numbers in dBFS, and how much of the track is left to play. Radio and live-input lanes have no countdown and show none.

The bar and the tick move continuously — they are the live meter — but the numbers settle a few times a second rather than every frame, because a figure redrawn sixty times a second cannot be read. Nothing is lost between refreshes: the peak figure reports the loudest the deck got since the last one, so a transient that came and went still shows up in it.

Drag the seam between the waves and the column to make the column wider or narrower; drag it right past the end — or right-click it — to hide the column altogether and give the whole band to the waves. The seam stays behind as a slim DIAGNOSTICS strip, and dragging it back to the left brings the column back at the width you left it. Hidden or shown, it is remembered between sessions.

Deck sources & radio

Each deck's SRC button shows what it is playing: IN (live input), CLIP (the deck player), GRID (the clip grid) or RAD (an internet radio stream). Left-click flips between live input and the player; right-click opens the full source menu:

  • LIVE INPUT — the device input this channel hears, picked by name: each open input, a stereo pair (1-2, 3-4), or Off. AUTO is input 1 for CH 1, input 2 for CH 2 and so on, so a laptop with one input feeds CH 1 only. The deck's readout says LIVE IN 1, or NO INPUT when the input it names is not open on the device; the REC menu shows the same choice for an audio take and reads in red when the take would record silence; the System check's Live inputs row says so too.
  • CLIP PLAYER — the ordinary deck face.
  • CLIP GRID — the 8-slot pad face; see Clip grid.
  • RADIO ▸ — a submenu listing your saved stations by name (tick = playing here), plus Browse stations… to find and save more.

CLIP PLAYER and CLIP GRID are two faces of one source, so switching between them never disturbs playback, and each deck remembers the face you left it on. Leaving RADIO stops the stream first.

A deck on IN shows the input, not the clip. The clip's controls step aside, and the deck shows what it hears: LIVE and the input's name, the input's own waveform scrolling past (the same picture as the WAVEFORM band, with the beat grid while the transport runs), and a level bar read before the fader, so you can see the input with the fader down. The bar's top turns red when the input reaches full scale. If a clip was playing, it is parked, and a line at the foot of the deck says so; SRC takes you back to it. With no input open, the deck says NO INPUT; right-click SRC to choose one.

Clip grid

Switching a playing deck to LIVE parks it where it was. Switch back and it comes in on the next bar line (with QUANT off, on the next beat), from the parked place, in step with the room; a stale launch is never fired late. A deck the Composer is playing comes back where the timeline is.

Every deck can wear a grid of 8 clip slots instead of its zoomed waveform: right-click SRC → CLIP GRID. The slim full-track overview underneath stays live for seeking and loops. The grid is a chooser for the deck's one player, not a second voice — so a launch is the usual quantized launch.

  • Fill a slot — drag a track onto it, or highlight a track in COLLECTION and tap an empty pad (that only files it, nothing launches, so a mis-tap can't interrupt the deck).
  • Tap a filled pad to launch it on the next quantize point; tap again to stop. Tapping during the load cancels the queued launch.
  • Right-click a pad for Assign selected library track, Loop on/off (per slot, kept through a reassign), Clear slot and Assign from file….
  • Pads outline when they hold the deck's clip, blink while armed and pulse on the beat while playing.
  • MIDI-learnable — every slot on every deck appears in the mapper as CH1 Grid Slot 1 … so a pad controller can fire them.

Grids are saved with the session. If a clip's file has moved or been deleted since you last played, the slot is cleared on load (the session reports how many items it couldn't restore), and tapping a pad whose file has vanished warns you without stopping the deck that's playing.

Stems

Cadence can split a track into drums, bass, other and vocals and play it back from those four parts — drop the vocal, keep the drums under the next tune, solo a bassline.

  • One-time setup — open SETTINGS and press Download model (316 MB). It fetches once and stays.
  • Only the tracks you choose: Cadence separates a track only when you ask. A separation takes a few minutes of the machine and keeps about 100 MB of stems per track, and some tracks are better left as they are. You can ask in four ways: a deck's STEMS button on a track that has none (below), a track's row menu in the Collection, Separate stems under SETTINGS → Library for tracks you add or scan, and Separate tracks as they load under Stems for every track.
  • STEMS on a track without stems: with the model installed, a deck whose track has no stems still shows its STEMS row. STEMS is live and the four chips are greyed. Click STEMS and Cadence asks Separate this track's stems?. Choose Separate and that one track separates on the deck, and STEMS switches on. Nothing starts without the click.
  • Separate a track as it loads: when Separate tracks as they load is on, or when you chose the track from its STEMS button, the separation runs on the deck. The separation starts where the deck will play (the top, a parked start or a running loop), works forward, and then fills in what lies behind. A hot cue or a loop jump into a part that is not separated yet moves the separation there. The stems are usable within a few seconds, and the finished stems are kept, so the next load of that track is instant. It is always a full-quality separation.
  • Separate ahead of time: in COLLECTION, right-click a track → Separate stems (several selected tracks queue up). To separate every track you add, turn on SETTINGS → Library → Separate stems (off by default): an import, a Scan and Analyse & section library then queue their tracks that have no stems. Results are cached, so a track is only ever separated once. A deck always goes first: while a deck separates its track, the queue pauses between two of its own steps.
  • Play them — a loaded track that has stems grows a STEMS row on its deck: the STEMS toggle plus four chips, DRM BSS OTH VOC. Lit = audible. Click a chip to mute it, right-click to solo (right-click again to release the solo). Stem edits are quantized: a chip click, a solo and the STEMS toggle itself all land on the next QUANT boundary and blink until they do, so a kill drops on the bar and not half a beat late — shift-click for instant, and a second click on a blinking chip takes it back out of the queue.
  • Instrumental and acapella: click VOC for the instrumental, right-click it for the acapella. Both land on the next QUANT boundary like any chip, and the same gesture again brings the whole track back.
  • Echo a stem out: press and hold a chip (or right-click the stem's name in the mixer's STEMS rows) and choose Echo out. The stem mutes on the next QUANT boundary, and its last beat repeats on the channel's delay and fades while the rest of the track plays on. Most people use it on the vocal. The send opens a beat and a half before the boundary and is fully open for the last beat, so the tail of the phrase is what repeats. With QUANT off or finer than a beat, the echo waits for the next beat, because an echo needs time to catch something. The echo uses the delay's own time and sound. Its feedback is yours, held between 0.35 and 0.60 while it rings, so it always dies away: at the default settings it is 60 dB down after about ten repeats. While the echo waits, the chip blinks like any queued mute. While it runs, the chip's send mark is filled. To cancel, choose Echo out again before the boundary: the stem keeps playing and what the echo already caught rings out. To bring the stem back later, click its chip. Your own DLY and VERB sends are never changed. Echo out works with key-lock on. It needs the delay switched on in the FX panel, and it is greyed with an external mixer, which has no Cadence delay.
  • Roll one stem: the same menu has a Roll row with three lengths, 1/4, 1/2 and 1 beat. Press and hold one: the stem repeats that part of the beat while the other three play on. Let go: the stem finishes its repeat and comes back exactly where it would have been, like the deck's own rolls. One stem rolls at a time. The chip's small square blinks while its stem rolls. On a track that is still separating, a roll starts only where the separation has finished; otherwise it does not start, and the deck says why.
  • From a controller: the mapping list has, for every deck and stem, CH n Vocals solo (acapella) (and Drums, Bass, Other solo), CH n Vocals echo out, and CH n Vocals roll 1/4, 1/2 and 1, which roll while the pad is held. The instrumental is the chip itself, CH n Vocals. These moves have no control of their own on screen, so learn them from the mapping list. The Launchpad surface has no free pad for them; map a controller of your own.
  • Read the STEMS button: it fills solid the moment you ask for stems, not when they finish loading, and the small square in its corner says where the deck actually is: hollow = off, blinking = the stems are not there yet, solid = they are playing. Off, the button is an outline; on, it is a filled block, so which state a deck is in reads across the booth. With all four stems up, a deck plays the original track exactly, so pressing STEMS changes nothing you can hear until you mute a stem.
  • While the track separates: a thin bar along the bottom edge of STEMS shows how far the separation has got, and its tooltip gives the percentage. The bar goes when the separation is done. STEMS works the whole time: press it early and the stems play wherever the separation has reached. Where it has not (right after a jump, or on a slow machine), the deck plays the track as it is, the square blinks and the chips turn quiet. When the separation is far enough ahead again, the stems come back by themselves. Both changes are a 50 ms fade, so nothing clicks.
  • See the vocal coming — a separated track draws a thin vocal lane along the bottom edge of its deck waveforms, on the overview strip and on the zoomed view above it. The lane is solid where someone sings, dim where the vocal is faint, and absent where nobody sings. On the zoomed view it arrives bars before the playhead, so you can time a mix around a vocal. It stays on the music under SYNC and on a warped grid. To hide it, right-click the overview strip and untick Show vocal lane. The choice applies to every deck.
  • Set each stem's level — drag a stem chip up or down to turn that stem up or down. A thin bar along the chip's bottom edge shows the level: full at 0 dB, half-way at -12 dB, empty when the stem is off. A chip at 0 dB shows no bar. Alt-click a chip to put it back to 0 dB. A small wobble while you click still mutes: a press becomes a level drag only after it has moved a few pixels. For a controller, map CH n Drums level (and Bass, Other, Vocals) in the mapping window; the knob travels like a channel fader. The level is yours alone: Assist's stem moves and the Composer's stem envelopes ride on top of it and never reset it. Loading another track or ejecting puts every stem back to 0 dB.
  • Stem automation from the Composer — a stem envelope drawn in the COMPOSER (the STEMS chip there) moves that stem while the set plays, and Perform shows it: the chip's bar and the mixer's stem row are lit up to what the stem plays and dim from there up to your own level, and the channel's strip lights S. Your level stays yours: a drag or a mapped knob still sets it, and the envelope works under it. Assist's stem moves show the same way. When a stem curve starts, STEMS switches on for that deck by itself if the track has stems, even on a lane with no clip. With STEMS off the chips still show the duck, because that is what STEMS will launch with.
  • With stems off, the chips are a preset — on a track that has stems, clicking a chip while STEMS is off only mutes or un-mutes that stem. It does not switch stems on, decode anything or consult the deck cap: dial in which stems you want and then press STEMS, and they launch together exactly as the row showed them. Nothing is audible yet, so these edits apply instantly instead of waiting for a bar — the row always reads as what the next STEMS press will sound like. While stems are off the chips are drawn quietly: the mute state is still readable, and the absence of the lit fill is what tells you nothing is affecting the audio yet. The mute set survives switching stems off and on — kill the vocal, drop stems for a bar, bring them back and the vocal is still killed. Loading another track or ejecting clears it, and it is never written to disk, so a fresh launch always starts all-audible.
  • Let Assist use them — when Stem transitions is on (the ASSIST features menu, on by default), the auto-DJ mixes with the parts instead of only the EQ: a bass swap trades the actual basslines on the swap bar, the incoming vocal is held back until the outgoing track is out of the way, a build-up strips the outgoing tune down to drums and bass under the riser, and when both tracks are separated it can hand over drums first, then bass — the way you would. Under the Gradual mix style it stages the whole hand-over part by part; see Assist. Decks without stems simply get the normal EQ transition; nothing needs arming, and your own chip mutes always win.
  • Tune the cost: SETTINGS → Stems. Separate tracks as they load is off by default: turn it on and every track without stems separates as it lands on a deck; turn it off again and any separation running on the decks stops and frees its memory. It used to be on by default. Because most sessions only held that old default, a session from before this change opens with the setting off, once; if you want it, turn it on again. The Clear stems cache button shows how much disk the stems use. Clearing is up to you: nothing deletes stems by itself. Stems on up to caps how many decks may hold stems at once (Auto reads your installed RAM and names the number it chose). Separation quality applies to Separate stems in the Collection; separating on load is always full. Separation speed (Gentle, Balanced or Fast) is how many cores a separation may use; it never touches playback. On Apple Silicon there is also an experimental CoreML toggle, off by default: where CoreML will not take the model Cadence separates on the CPU and says so, so it can never make a separation fail.
Memory, not magic. A stems deck keeps the whole track's four parts in RAM, around 210 MB for a 5-minute track, so the number of stems decks is capped, and asking for one past the cap is refused with a toast that names it. A deck separating its track counts too, and holds about half as much again while it separates. A deck past the cap waits: its STEMS row shows greyed with the reason, and it starts separating when another deck lets go. Change the cap under SETTINGS → Stems. Key-lock works with stems: a stemmed deck holds its pitch under SYNC exactly like any other.

Assist (auto-DJ)

Press ASSIST and Cadence mixes the night itself: it picks the next track, loads it on a free deck, matches tempo and key, waits for the right phrase, and runs the whole hand-over, EQ, stems and effects, while you watch, grab a fader when you want to, or step away. Right-click ASSIST for its menu. A one-line strip under the top row says what it is doing: now > next, the move it chose, how many bars are left, and the set's arc.

  • Start. With a deck playing, Assist mixes out of it on the next good phrase. With nothing playing it picks an opener itself, a track with a long intro, mid energy and, from a playlist, the playlist's dominant key, and starts it on the bar. Hold pauses the mixing where it stands, Mix now goes at the next phrase, Skip the next pick arms another track, Undo last mix brings the previous track back up.
  • Mode preset. Match arms the pick, beat- and key-matches it, and leaves the fader to you. Blend runs the crossfade and the bass swap. Full adds the effects and chains forever.
  • Mix style, Gradual by default. Gradual is long and smooth: an eight-bar floor on every overlap, EQ blends of twenty bars and filter sweeps of thirty, a gentle EQ notch, the bass swapped late, no hard cuts, and with stems on both decks a layered hand-over: the new kit comes in first and alone, the bass crosses over a bar at the swap, the old kit leaves a bar later, the melodies cross after that, and the vocals keep the clash guard's rules. Nothing moves twice and no two parts move on the same beat. Balanced is the four-bar middle; Quick is short with cuts allowed.
  • Transition pace sets when the mix-out comes: Long holds a track to its end, Short mixes earlier, Frequent is about five minutes a track. Loop blends adds the occasional minute-long loop-and-blend.
  • Energy arc. Auto shapes the night from the clock, warm-up, build, peak and close, over the set length you give it (60, 90 or 120 minutes, or Endless for a gentle drift). Warm-up, Peak-time and Closing hold one phase.
  • Key match: Prefer favours harmonic neighbours on the Camelot wheel, Strict allows nothing else. Tempo: Locked holds one BPM across the set and stretches each track to it; Adapt lets the master follow each song, and the bend now takes the whole overlap, half as much again under Gradual, so the room hears one slow glide across the hand-over and never a tempo that settles while the old track plays alone.
  • The moves. Assist chooses from EQ blend, bass swap, filter fade, slow filter sweep, loop blend, roll-out, echo out, build-hold (the incoming's build loops under the crossfade and releases onto its drop on a phrase downbeat), stem swap (drums on the phrase, bass a bar later), the breakdown swap (two breakdowns lined up, the hand-over in the mutual quiet) and, at peak time, a cut on the one from drop to drop. The choice reads key fit, energy, grid trust, the arc and what played last, and the strip names it.
  • Stems. With Stem transitions on (Features, on by default) the parts do the work: a real bass swap trades the basslines, the incoming vocal waits until the outgoing one has stopped singing (measured from the separated vocal, bar by bar, with the blind schedule as the worst case), a build strips the outgoing to drums and bass, and a bad key fit keeps the two melodies apart. Decks without stems get the EQ transition; your own stem mutes always win.
  • Features. Auto-select, beat-match, key-match, auto-blend, bass-swap EQ, FX, continuous, drop-cut style, a generated MIDI layer, structure-aware entry and exit, grid-trust gating, mood match and stem transitions, each its own switch.
  • Source. All songs, or one playlist, in order or picked. The Next-Up queue (from the phone remote or a right-click) is mixed first, in order. Guest requests from the guest link wait for your accept.
  • Around the set. Pre-flight checks the rig in nine rows before you start. The setlist writes every track that reached the main output, with its tempo, key and how it left, and a Tonight view lists the night. With an external mixer Assist picks and you mix.

Assist writes stem gains, never stem mutes; it never arms stems on a deck you armed by hand; and every ramp it runs eases in and out, so no move starts or stops with a jerk.

Transport, sync & quantize

One quantize grid

The quantize setting (SETTINGS → Interface → Quantize: Off, 1/16 … 4 Bars) governs everything: clip launches and stops, seeks, beat-jumps, quantized mutes, and loop snapping — loop in/out points land on the chosen grid, so loop lengths come out quantized automatically. "Off" means immediate timing with beat-snapped loops.

Tempo

Typed or dragged tempo changes glide over several seconds rather than snapping (SYNC'd decks bend along smoothly). →BPM on a strip glides the master to that track's native tempo. Tap with T. Range 60–180 BPM.

Sync outputs

Cadence is a clock source: MIDI clock out (pick the port in SETTINGS → Clock & Sync, beside out; free-run keeps clock flowing while stopped) and OSC for visuals and modules (see OSC). Hosted plugins read the master playhead, so sequencer plugins run in lockstep.

Following a MIDI clock

Cadence can also follow another clock: a drum machine a live act starts from, a DAW over IAC or a network session, or another performer's rig. In SETTINGS → Clock & Sync, set Clock to Follow and the input the clock arrives on. Internal is Cadence leading, as before. The input can be your controller's own device.

  • Tempo. Cadence measures the clock's tempo from its ticks and plays at it. It locks in about one beat. The BPM field then reads MIDI in place of BPM. The number in it, the tempo your synced decks are rendered for, moves only when the clock changes by more than 0.1 BPM. So a jittery clock never makes Cadence render your decks again.
  • Beat. The clock's Start puts bar 1 on its first beat. Continue after a song position puts Cadence at that bar. A deck armed for the next bar starts on the clock's bar 1. Cadence lines up the beat you hear with the beat the gear plays: it allows for its own output latency and for the wait of latency compensation. If the gear's own sound is late, set Offset (beside Free-running clock) to its latency in ms.
  • No jumps. Cadence never jumps a deck that plays. It moves the bar counter by whole beats, and it closes the rest by playing a little faster or slower for a moment, at most 4 %, the way you nudge a deck.
  • Transport. Start and Continue start Cadence's transport. Stop stops it, and your clock out sends Stop to the gear after Cadence. A deck that is playing keeps playing: a clock message never stops the music.
  • What switches off. While Cadence follows, the tempo is the clock's. The BPM field takes no typed tempo, and T, a deck's →BPM and a radio deck's one-shot SYNC are greyed with the reason. Set Clock to Internal to set the tempo yourself again.
  • Clock out. Cadence's own clock out keeps running from its transport, so gear after Cadence follows too. A clock out to the device the clock comes in from would loop back to Cadence, so Settings greys that pairing and says why.
  • When the clock is lost. If the ticks stop and no Stop came, Cadence keeps the last tempo and beat and plays on. The BPM field reads LOST, and the system check's clock row turns red. When the clock comes back, Cadence follows it again with no jump. If the clock changed its tempo while it was away, nobody can count the ticks it played, so Cadence takes the new tempo and keeps its own beat until the next Start or Continue.

Loops & loop rolls

Loops

  • Drag across a track overview to set a loop (edges snap to the quantize grid); drag the IN/OUT handles to resize — changes are adopted on the next beat, the music never jumps mid-cycle.
  • LOOP N on the strip loops N beats from the current beat; /2/x2 resize live, and the counter follows.
  • Saved loops (from the section editor or rekordbox import) appear dimmed in the overview and auto-engage when the playhead reaches them — prepare a "hold here" loop at the end of a track and it can't be missed.
  • If you drag a loop ahead of the playhead, the deck plays into it and then loops — it never yanks backwards.
  • Saved loops on the deck: LOOP+ saves the loop you are playing as the next saved loop, and each saved loop gets a chip, LOOP A to LOOP H (the ones that do not fit are under +N). Click a chip to loop it from its start. Right-click it to rename it, to delete it, or to Set to the playing loop: the saved loop takes the bars you are looping now. A loop you only play is not saved over a saved one: a saved loop changes only when you change it, here or in the Composer, where saved loops show as loop cues on the track's clips.

Loop rolls

The 1/4 1/2 1 2 pads are momentary: hold to stutter a grid-aligned window while the real position keeps running underneath (watch the ghost playhead); release and the current repeat finishes on its boundary, then playback snaps back exactly in time. The pad stays lit until the release lands.

Slip & pitch play

Slip

SLIP sits in the jump row, between the jump arrows and CUE+. Lit, it is on for that deck. With SLIP on, the track keeps its own time underneath what you do. Loop it, hold a hot cue, hold a jump: when you let go, the deck comes back to where the track would be by now, as if it had played on. It comes back on the next beat, or on the next QUANT step when QUANT is finer than a beat, through the same short crossfade every jump has.

  • Loops. A loop you start with SLIP on runs over the track's own time: LOOP N, a saved loop, or a loop drawn on the overview. Exit it and the deck comes back to where the track would be.
  • Hot cues. With SLIP on a hot cue pad is held. The cue plays while the pad is down, and the deck comes back when you let go. A quick tap plays the cue for one beat.
  • Jumps. < and > hold the same way. The deck plays from the jump while you hold, and comes back when you let go.
  • Rolls worked this way before SLIP existed, and they still do, with SLIP on or off.
  • See the way back. While a slip runs, a pale line on the overview and on the zoomed view marks where the track is by now. That is where the deck will come back to. The same line shows under a roll.
  • What lets a slip go. Turning SLIP off, stopping the deck, or a plain move (a click on the overview, a section chip) ends the slip where the deck is. Nothing jumps back then. Loops made by Assist and by the Composer are never slips.
  • SLIP is saved per deck with the session. With SLIP off, every pad and button does exactly what it did before.

Pitch play

Pitch play plays one hot cue at eight keys, like the pitch-play pads of other DJ tools. The tempo stays where it is. Only the pitch moves, through key-lock.

  • Start it. Right-click a hot cue pad and choose Pitch play this cue. The cue row turns into eight pads labelled with their keys: -4 to +3 at first, with the track's own key on the fifth pad. The pad whose key you hear is lit.
  • Play it. Press a pad. The deck jumps to the cue on the next beat and plays it at that key. The key stays until you press another pad. With SLIP on a pad is held: the cue plays at its key while you hold, and when you let go the deck comes back to the track's own time at its own key.
  • Choose the pads. Right-click a pad. Pads picks the range: -7 to 0, -4 to +3, 0 to +7, or a major or minor scale from the track's key up an octave. Play cue picks another cue. The range is saved with the session.
  • End it. Choose End pitch play on the same menu. The deck goes back to its own key on the next beat, and the music does not jump. Loading a track or ejecting ends it too.
  • With a key shift. The pads are relative to the deck's own key. With KEY+2 and pads 0 to +7, the top pad plays +9. Keys stop at ±12 semitones.
  • KEY must be on. Pitch play moves the pitch through key-lock, so with KEY off a pad does nothing and the deck says why. While the mode is on, a short SYNC'd loop or a track on a warped grid is rendered for key-lock. If you press a pad before that is ready, the deck asks you to press again.

From a controller

  • CH n Slip switches SLIP on and off.
  • CH n Pitch pad 1 to 8 play the chosen cue at each pad's key while the pad is held.
  • CH n Pitch play cue picks the cue. Each press takes the next saved cue, and a press after the last one ends pitch play. A pitch pad pressed with no cue picked takes the first one.
  • With SLIP on, a mapped hot cue or jump holds until its pad lets go. With pitch play on, the deck's hot cue mappings play the pitch pads, as the cue row does on screen.

On the Launchpad

The Launchpad has a third page for pitch play. The top scene button steps through MIX (green), PERFORM (amber) and PITCH (red). With an X-Session Pro on the bus it flips between the trigger page and PITCH.

  • Each deck keeps its two columns. The top four rows are its eight pitch pads, the key rising upward. The pad you hear is lit bright, and the track's own key is amber.
  • The bottom four rows are the deck's hot cues 1 to 8. Press one to choose the cue the pads play. It lights amber.
  • The round buttons along the top: the first four are the loop rolls, as on PERFORM. The last four switch SLIP for decks 1 to 4.
  • Leaving the page ends pitch play on the decks it played, and they go back to their own key.
  • With SLIP on, the hot cues and the jumps on the PERFORM page are held too.

What the third page costs: from PERFORM back to MIX takes two presses of the page button instead of one.

Key-lock & key matching

When SYNC bends a track's tempo, key-lock keeps its pitch constant — realtime, full-length tracks, all four decks at once (the engine has been measured at ~5% DSP with all four stretching at a 128-sample buffer).

  • KEY lit = key-lock on (default). Unlit = vinyl-style: pitch follows tempo.
  • Right-click KEY — shift the track's key by semitones (−6…+6), or Match CH n: Cadence reads both detected keys and applies the shortest shift to make them harmonically compatible (minor keys are compared through their relative major). The button shows the active shift ("KEY+2").
  • The key shift resets when a new track loads.
  • Pitch play plays one hot cue at eight keys from eight pads. See Slip & pitch play.
Harmonic browsing: in the COLLECTION, tracks whose key is compatible with the playing deck (same key, relative, or a wheel neighbour) tint green — pick your next track by colour. The Key column shows both the key name and the wheel code ("Am  8A").

Collection

Scanning

Scan analyses tracks (several in parallel): tempo and a bar-accurate beatgrid (comb-filtered detection with a kick-weighted downbeat vote), the musical key (tuning-corrected chromagram — works on A≠440 masters), and integrated loudness for auto-gain. The Grid column shows confidence; Verified means a human (or rekordbox) confirmed it.

Search

The search box filters as you type. Every space-separated term must match: words search the title, artist, album, genre and key, plain numbers match BPM ±1, and 120-126 filters a BPM range. Example: techno 7A 122-126. Esc clears.

MATCH, beside the search box, shows only the tracks that mix with the deck on the master: a compatible key (the same Camelot number, or a neighbour on the wheel) and a BPM within 6 %, at half, normal or double time. It follows the master as the mix moves on, and the count beside it says what it holds against (“12 / 840 tracks (MATCH 8A 124.0)”). A track with no key or no BPM is left out until you scan it. With nothing on the master, MATCH shows every track. The search still works inside it.

Played tonight. A track that played on the master for 30 seconds is marked in every view: its row dims and its title gets a check. The Tonight view, under COLLECTION, lists the night first play first, so you can see your set so far and play something again on purpose. Tonight’s setlist... in the PLAYLIST menu opens the full log with its exports.

Titles and artists from the files

Cadence reads each file’s own tags when you add it: the title, artist, album, genre and key, from MP3 and AAC (ID3), M4A, FLAC, Ogg, WAV and AIFF. The Collection has an Artist column, search finds artists and albums, and the decks, the setlist and the phone remote show “Artist - Title”. Tracks you added before this are read once, in the background, after Cadence starts. Only blanks are filled, and a title changes only while it is still the file’s name, so a title you typed stays. A file that iCloud keeps only in the cloud is left alone, because reading it would download it. To take the file’s values over yours, right-click and choose Read tags from the file; ⌘Z takes it back. Edit track info… edits the title, artist, album, key and genre. A rekordbox import keeps the artist in its own field, and the rekordbox export writes it back.

Rating, colour, comment and energy

Click a star in the Rating column to rate a track from one to five; click its own rating again to clear it. Right-click → Rating or Colour marks every selected track at once, and ⌘Z takes it back. The colours are rekordbox’s eight, and the narrow column beside # shows them. Comment is your own note; Edit track info… edits it, search finds it, and a file’s comment tag fills it when it is blank (Mixed In Key writes the key and energy there). Energy is the scan’s measure of how hard the whole track drives, from 1 to 10. Every one of these columns sorts. A rekordbox import brings its ratings, colours and comments, and the export writes them back. Right-click the column headers to hide a column you do not use; it stays hidden when you switch views.

Playlists & the right-click menu

Build playlists with New Playlist; multi-select tracks and right-click to add them. The right-click menu also offers: Load playing (fader down) (audition: the deck starts, channel on, fader at the bottom — ride it in by hand), Scan, Edit beatgrid…, Edit sections…, Edit track info…, Open in Editor, and removal options. The LOAD 1–4 buttons glow with each channel's live level so you never load over a playing deck.

Keys in the Collection. ⌘F goes to the search box; ↓ or Return there moves into the results. Return loads the selected track (in Radio it plays the station, in Samples it does what a double-click does). Esc clears the selection. Delete, the REMOVE button and the menu’s Delete from the Collection all ask the same question before they delete tracks that carry analysis or marks; in a playlist, Delete and REMOVE only take the tracks off the playlist. The Key column sorts round the Camelot wheel, so neighbours sit together. A column keeps the width you give it when you switch views. The Grid column says Locked for a grid you locked in the Track Editor. The Launchpad’s view arrows step over Sample Browser and Radio.

Sorting and selecting. Click a column header to sort by it; click # to go back to the view’s own order (the Collection’s, or the playlist’s, which you can then drag into a new order). The selected tracks stay selected through a sort or a search, and the BPM box always shows and edits the selected track. A track whose grid is locked keeps its BPM: unlock it in the Track Editor first. Keys can be typed as Am, A minor, C major or as a Camelot code such as 8A. Every edit in the Collection, an import included, is one step of ⌘Z.

Preview from the waveform. Click anywhere along a track’s Wave cell to hear it in your headphones from that point; click again to jump. The cue-coloured line shows where it plays. Esc stops it.

Drag several tracks. Select tracks and drag one of them: the whole selection goes. Dropped on a Composer lane, they land one after another from the drop point, each on the bar after the last one ends, and stay selected so you can move them together. Several files from Finder land the same way. A deck takes the track you grabbed. Dropped on the playlist box above the list, the tracks ask which playlist to join, or start a new one.

Loads never cut a deck on air without asking. LOAD 1–4, a double-click, Load playing and a radio station all ask before they replace a deck that is playing on the master. A double-click on a station goes to the deck a track would take, not always CH 1.

Preview in cue never takes a deck you are using. When a deck is free, the preview plays there, in your headphones and beatmatched to the mix as that deck plays it, and the next preview reuses it. When every deck holds a track, the preview plays on its own, at the track’s own tempo, and no deck is touched. A preview deck you put on air is yours from then on: the next preview leaves it alone.

Library folders

The Collection's + menu → Library folders... keeps a list of the folders you keep music in and scans them. It shows three lists. New: audio files in those folders that are not in the Collection yet; import the ones you pick, or all of them. Duplicates: the same audio more than once, wherever it is, such as a copy in a second folder or a renamed copy; each set is listed with which copies are in the Collection, and you can remove the extras from the Collection or show one in Finder. Missing: Collection tracks whose file is gone. When a file moved inside your folders, the scan finds it by its name and length, and the row says where it is now. Relink found points those tracks at their files, with their grids, cues, loops and playlists. Find in folder... looks for the missing files in any folder you pick, such as a renamed drive. A clip in the open composition and a pad on a deck’s grid follow a relinked file. A file with two equal candidates is left for you: right-click it in the Collection and choose Locate file.... You can also remove the missing tracks you pick, or all of them. A removal is the Collection's own delete, so Cmd+Z brings tracks back. Files are never deleted from your disk. The folder list is saved with the Collection, and the dialog scans again whenever it opens or you change something.

Export to rekordbox

PLAYLIST → Export to rekordbox XML... writes your whole Collection and every playlist to one file (by default ~/Music/Cadence rekordbox.xml). Each track carries its beatgrid (a warped grid with its anchors), its hot cues A to H in their colours, its saved loops as memory loops, its sections as named memory cues, and its key, BPM, genre and length; a title written Artist - Title becomes the two fields. Your playlists sit in a folder named Cadence. A track whose file has moved or gone is left out, and the toast says how many. In rekordbox, open the file under Preferences → Advanced → Database → rekordbox xml, then drag the tracks and playlists from the rekordbox xml tree into your collection; from there they export to USB for CDJs as usual.

Find mashups

Right-click a track and open Find mashups: the tracks in your Collection that sit with it, best first. A partner has to share the key or a neighbour on the Camelot wheel (or the relative major or minor), and a tempo within 8 percent at one, half or double time; each row shows the partner's key and BPM, the ratio and stretch the decks will use, and the two roles. The roles come from the stems: a track whose vocal stem sings over most of its length is a vocal, one that hardly sings an instrumental, and the pair that ranks first is a vocal on an instrumental. A track without stems still pairs by key and tempo and reads role unknown, below the pairs whose roles contrast. Pick a row and both tracks load onto two free decks, SYNC on, parked so the voice lands on the drop: the instrumental waits at its first section named Drop, the vocal track at the bar where its singing starts. A track without a drop section or a vocal lane waits at its first downbeat. Press PLAY on both, and mute the instrumental's own vocals with its stem chips.

Recordings

Pick Recordings in the Collection's list (under COLLECTION) to see everything Cadence recorded, newest first: the AUDIO takes you record on a channel and the MASTER recordings made with REC. A MIDI take saved as a .mid file in the takes folder is listed too, as MIDI. Each row shows its kind, name, length and date. Load a recording like a track: the LOAD buttons, a double-click, Return, or the row menu (Load to CH n; a MIDI take plays into the channel's instrument). Drag it to a deck or the Composer. The list reads the folders the recorders write into, so a new take appears the moment it is saved and a file you delete in Finder is gone from it. Recordings is not a playlist: it cannot be renamed or deleted, and it has no delete of its own. Reveal in Finder in the row menu shows the file.

A track's own MIDI and OSC layers

A track can carry its own MIDI layer (notes drawn in the Track Editor that play into the deck's instrument) and its own OSC layer (envelopes sent on the module bus). They travel with the track to any deck. A track that carries one shows the channel's small MIDI or OSC mark at the right end of its title, and the title's tooltip says what it carries, for example MIDI layer: 3 notes on channel 1. OSC layer: 1 envelope. Right-click the track: Edit layers… opens the Track Editor on the layer, and Remove this track's MIDI layer… or OSC layer… removes one after asking. The track itself is not changed, a deck playing it stops the layer at once, and Cmd+Z in the Collection restores it.

Importing music

  • Add Files — WAV, AIFF, FLAC, MP3, OGG, M4A and AAC, single files or whole folders (hidden files and macOS “._” files are skipped). A track’s title comes from its file name. An import that finds no audio says so, and the whole import is one step of ⌘Z.
  • Import XML — a rekordbox collection export (File → Export Collection in xml format). Brings in tracks, your curated beatgrids (marked Verified), up to eight hot cues (A to H), saved loops, and the playlist tree. Existing tracks are updated, not duplicated; a summary reports anything missing on disk.

Imported grids are trusted as-is; keys and loudness still benefit from a Cadence scan.

Grid & section editors

Beatgrid editor

Grid opens the beatgrid editor: set the tempo and drag the downbeat by eye against the waveform. Applying marks the grid Verified and pushes it live to any loaded deck. The /2 and x2 buttons next to the BPM field fix octave-doubled or halved detections.

Section editor

Sections opens the section/cue/loop editor (it deliberately never touches the beatgrid). Add any number of coloured, labelled sections snapped to the grid — they show up in the collection previews, the mixer overviews and the Composer timeline, so you read a track's structure everywhere. Also set up to four hot cues and a saved loop here.

Composer

A DAW-style timeline for preparing a set. Drag tracks from the docked collection onto a lane — each becomes a full-length clip so you can see where it ends and line up the next one. When the master playhead crosses a clip, its deck launches.

  • The toolbar: COMPOSITIONS, then the clip tools (the four arrows to move, the scissors for cutting, the loop arrows for a loop region and the flag for cues and loop cues), then what you draw, in the order a mix is made: VOL, STEMS, EQ, FILT, DLY, VERB, then MACRO, MIDI and OSC, then WRITE and CURVE. The tool or target you are on is filled with the accent colour, so one chip reads as selected at a time. Every chip is one click from every other: from an automation target, the scissors, the loop arrows, the flag or MIDI take you back to the clips with that tool. Hover a chip for what it does; the manual is the place for the rest. The waveform colour switch, FREQ, is in the top bar, as in Perform.
  • Clips: with the move tool (the four arrows), drag a clip to move it and drag an edge to trim it; the scissors split a clip in two. While you drag, the clip moves on screen only: nothing starts, stops or jumps until you let go. Then the decks follow the timeline: a clip moved under the playhead plays from the next bar line, and a playing clip moved away from it stops its deck. A tag over the clip shows where it lands, as bar.beat.sixteenth. A clip catches the start or end of any other clip within 8 pixels, and a line down the lanes marks the edge it caught, so the next track lands exactly where the last one ends. Drag to the left or right edge of the view and it scrolls, faster the closer you go. A clip's loop region moves with it.
  • Several clips at once: drag across empty lane space to draw a box; every clip it touches is selected. Shift-click (or ⌘-click) a clip to add it or take it out, and ⌘A selects every clip. Drag any selected clip and they all move by the same amount. The move stops when the earliest one reaches bar 1, and goes in whole bars while one of them plays, so its deck stays on the downbeat. A click on one clip of the group selects it alone; Esc, or a click on empty space, clears the selection. Copy, cut, paste, duplicate and delete act on all of them: a pasted or duplicated group keeps its spacing and its lanes. To scroll the timeline by dragging, hold ⌘ while you drag empty space; the scrollbar, Shift+wheel and a trackpad scroll too.
  • Copy by dragging, nudge with the keys: hold Alt while you drag a clip (or a selection) and you drag a copy; the originals stay where they are, and the copies end up selected. The left and right arrow keys move the selection one grid step, or one bar while one of its clips plays.
  • The grid follows QUANT: at QUANT one beat the lanes show a line on every beat, and as you zoom out the lines thin out to every other line, then every bar, and so on, so they never crowd. What you drag lands on the lines you see: clips, their edges, loop regions, section ranges and automation points. Phrases (every four bars) draw strongest. With QUANT off the grid is a sixteenth of a bar.
  • Live clips: a deck playing in Perform shows on its lane as a clip, tagged LIVE, where the deck really is: it follows a hot cue, a loop or a seek. Touch it (click, drag, cut, loop or right-click) and it becomes a real clip in the set at that moment, already playing, with no relaunch. Untouched, it is not saved. A clip of the same track under the playhead takes its place.
  • MIDI clips show their notes right on the timeline, as audio clips show their waveform: each note where it plays, over the clip's own pitch range, brighter the harder it was played, and repeated lap by lap where the clip loops, with a faint line at each lap. A lane that holds only MIDI gives its clips the whole lane, and its header reads MIDI; a lane with audio clips keeps its MIDI clips in a strip underneath. Double-click a MIDI clip to edit its notes.
  • A track's own layers: a track can carry its own MIDI notes and OSC envelopes (made in the track editor), and they play on any deck the track is loaded on. A clip of such a track shows a MIDI and an OSC mark after its name, and its lane header lights the same marks. The notes show in the lane's MIDI strip under the clip, marked TRACK MIDI, exactly where the deck plays them. In OSC mode the envelopes show on the clip, dashed, in their own colour, with their address; the lane's own OSC envelope is drawn over them and is the one you edit there. Right-click the clip for Track's MIDI layer or Track's OSC layer: edit it in the track editor, or remove it from the track (the Collection asks first, and Cmd+Z in the Collection brings it back).
  • The deck on the timeline: the deck's live position is mirrored on the timeline, exactly: the clip's playhead, its automation and its loops follow the deck. A deck holds at most ten minutes of a track, so a clip of a longer track shows the rest hatched: the deck stops there.
  • Clip waveforms: each clip shows its track's waveform at the detail the zoom allows, from a whole set on one screen down to single kicks. The light outer shape is the peak; the solid core is the average level, so a drop reads as a thick band and a breakdown as a thin one. With FREQ off, the track's sections colour the waveform and its background, with a line and the section name where each one starts, as in PERFORM. With FREQ on, the waveform is tinted by bass, mids and highs. A trimmed clip shows only the part it plays, and a track with a tempo map is drawn through that map, so each bar of the waveform sits under the bar of the timeline where it plays. The track playing on a deck with no clip under the playhead shows dimmed on its lane, from the playhead to its end. FREQ sits beside the zoom buttons; on a very narrow Composer it is the first button to go, and PERFORM's FREQ, SETTINGS and a MIDI mapping still switch it.
  • Future loops: with the LOOP tool, drag a region on a clip — when the song reaches it, the deck stays in that loop (it pulses azure while engaged; edits to an engaged loop apply live). Drag across one of the song's sections and the region's edges catch the section's edges, so the loop is that section whatever QUANT is. A loop made in Perform shows on the clip that is playing on that deck, marked PERFORM, and follows every change live: /2, x2, a new in or out point, an exit. If the clip's own loop is playing, /2 and x2 in Perform resize the clip's loop too. A deck you loop in Perform is not stopped at its clip's end. With the LOOP tool, drag either end of a Perform loop on the clip to change it; the deck follows at once. To end it, right-click the clip → Clear the Perform loop (when the clip has a loop region of its own, the same item reads Clear loop region and clears that).
  • Cues and loop cues: a track's hot cues show on its clips as numbered flags along the top edge, and its saved loops (loop cues) as coloured bands along the bottom edge with their letter, each in its colour and with its name. They are the same cues and loops as in Perform: change one here and the pads, the loop chips and the overview change with it. Drag a flag to move a cue. Drag the end of a band to make a loop cue longer or shorter, or its letter to move the whole loop. Everything lands on the grid. Right-click a flag or a letter to rename it (type a name, or take one of the eight quick names), to give a cue a colour, to loop the clip on a loop cue's bars (Loop it here) or to delete it. Right-click a clip to add a cue or a 4-bar loop cue where you clicked, to save its loop region (or the Perform loop) as a loop cue, or to reach every cue and loop cue of the track by name under Cues and Loop cues, including one another flag hides. When a deck is looping on a saved loop and you move that loop, the deck moves with it; delete it and the loop ends.
  • The MARKS tool (the flag beside the loop arrows) is for working on marks. Click a clip and a hot cue lands there; drag across a clip and a loop cue covers the bars you crossed, catching a song section's edges as the loop tool does. Before you click, a dashed line and a faint flag with the next cue's number show where it will land; while you drag, the new band shows in the colour it will take, with a tag giving its bars and its length. Marks are easier to catch with this tool: a cue by its flag or anywhere down its line, a loop cue anywhere along its band. Clips do not move with it, so nothing is dragged by accident, and a drag on an empty lane still scrolls the view. CLEAR is off with MARKS, because a track's marks are shared with Perform: delete one from its own right-click menu.
  • Automation: VOL, FILT, DLY, VERB and OSC draw an envelope per lane, played back into the channel during the set (value labels show dB etc.; FILT has a centre line). MACRO draws a mapped macro in its home lane (every macro on every page: an envelope belongs to its macro, not to the page on screen; right-click lists them by page), and STEMS one stem's level per lane. Each lane header shows letters for the envelopes it holds: V F D R, and S for stems. Click a letter to draw that envelope on that lane. While you draw stems, the header shows four fixed slots, D B O V, so one click switches stem; STEMS itself opens the stem you drew last. Hover a letter to see what it is and how many points it has. CLEAR offers this target on one lane or on all lanes, all four stems (or all macros), every envelope on one lane, and All automation. Each row shows its point count, and each clear is one undo step. All automation asks first; it leaves clips and MIDI clips alone, and every stem goes back to full level.
  • EQ automation: EQ draws one EQ band per lane: LOW, MID or HIGH. Click it to open the band you drew last and again for the next; right-click lists each band with its lanes and points. In EQ mode the lane header shows L M H, so one click switches band, and a lane with EQ curves shows E among its letters. The bottom of the lane is a kill, the guide line is flat (0 dB) and the top is +6 dB. A hand on the strip's EQ moves the curve with it, as it does for volume. CLEAR offers all three bands at once.
  • Stem automation: STEMS draws one stem's level per lane. The stem follows the curve between its first and last point and is at full level outside it. When the curve starts, the deck's STEMS switches on if its track has stems. A lane whose curve cannot act says why at its bottom edge while you draw stems: no track on the deck, no stems for the track, or the stems cap is full. A lane with no clip follows the master clock, not the song. When the deck plays a track there, the lane offers Place the track here (also in the lane's right-click menu): one click turns the live track into a clip where it stands, and from then on the curve follows the song.
  • Jump with the ruler: click the bar ruler to move the playhead to the start of the bar under the pointer. While the set plays, the jump waits for the next bar line and lands on it, so every deck that keeps playing stays on the beat and the Composer's lanes start their clips in place. At QUANT 2 or 4 bars it waits for the next phrase line instead, so the phrase stays in step. A flag on the ruler shows the target bar and the bars still to wait. Click another bar to change the target. Esc, or a right-click on the flag, cancels it; stopping the set cancels it too. Press and drag to pick a bar before you let go. While the set is stopped, the playhead moves at once. A lane whose clip starts after the target bar stops its deck when the jump lands, whatever the clip’s end rule or the deck’s LOOP says: the clip has not started there yet. The ruler is locked while a live set runs from the decks, while Assist mixes, while a take counts in or records, and while Cadence follows an external clock; hover LOCKED to see which. An external drum machine or sequencer on the clock gets the new position on the jump's own sample.
  • Lock the playhead: the padlock at the right end of the ruler locks the playhead while you perform. A click on the ruler, Space and PLAY/STOP then leave it where it is, so a stray click cannot move or stop the set. Shift+Space still stops everything. Click the padlock again to unlock it. The lock stays on after a restart, and you can map it to a controller button in MAP mode. When another lock holds the ruler, such as a live set running from the decks, the padlock shows closed and dark, and a click on it says why.
  • A jump in Perform moves the composition: while the Composer plays, a click that jumps a deck playing one of its clips (on the overview, the track lanes at the top of Perform, a section button or the phone remote) moves the whole composition with it. The master playhead goes, on the next bar line, to the bar where that clip holds the bar you clicked, and every lane follows, so the other clips, the automation and the tempo stay in line with the deck. A click on a part of the track its clip does not hold moves only the deck, and a toast says so. With the padlock on, the jump is refused. When no Composer playback is running, or the deck plays outside the composition, a click moves only the deck, as before. Hot cues and loops are deck gestures and move only the deck.
  • Cycle a range: hold Alt and drag along the ruler to draw a cycle, in whole bars. While the set plays inside it, the playhead goes back to its start when it reaches its end, with the ruler's beat-safe jump, so every lane plays the start again and every deck stays on the beat. Past its end the set plays on. The cycle shows as a band along the top of the ruler, filled while it is on: drag an end to resize it, drag the band to move it, click it to switch it on or off. Shift-drag a range and press ⌘L to cycle it; ⌘L again switches the cycle off. The ruler’s right-click menu has the same commands. The cycle is saved with the composition, keeps cycling under the padlock, and an export plays the set straight through.
  • The tempo lane: right-click the ruler and pick Show the tempo lane to open a strip under the ruler for the master tempo over the set. Click it to add a point; the point starts at the tempo the set already has there. Drag a point up or down to change its tempo, one BPM a pixel, or a tenth of one with Shift; drag it sideways to move it on the grid. Double-click a point to type a tempo; right-click it to delete it, or the strip to clear or hide the lane. Between two points the tempo ramps; before the first and after the last it holds. While the Composer plays, the lane sets the master tempo, so a hand on the BPM goes back to the curve; while stopped, it sets the tempo where the playhead is. A live set run from the decks and an external MIDI clock keep their own tempo. The lane is saved with the composition, a section’s move, duplicate or delete carries its points, and an export follows it. Hidden, it still applies.
  • Back to the start: Home takes the playhead to bar 1 (at once while stopped, on the next bar line while playing). In the ruler’s right-click menu, STOP returns the playhead to where PLAY started makes every stop go back to the bar you pressed PLAY on, as a DAW can.
  • Start and stop: Space or PLAY starts the timeline from the playhead; press again to stop it. The stop stops every deck with it, so the set is silent and the ruler is free to jump.
  • Navigation: +/− zoom (or ⌘-scroll, which zooms in step with how far you scroll, so a trackpad swipe zooms gently), NOW keeps the view following the playhead, and each lane glows with its channel's level. Each lane header is a small channel strip: the channel's name on top, a vertical volume fader (the channel's own fader) beside a live meter, and the lane's marks and automation letters on the left. The channel's plugins are listed there in signal-flow order, with a line between the ones before the fader and the ones after it. Click a plugin to open it, drag it up or down to change the order (drop it below the line to put it after the fader; an instrument always stays before it), and right-click it for the same menu as in Perform. Drag the line between the headers and the timeline to make them wider (80 to 240 pixels).

Capture a live set

Turn on SETTINGS → LIBRARY → Also save REC as a set, and while REC records the master, Cadence also writes what you play into a Composer set. It is off by default: REC on its own only records into the Recordings folder. Stop REC and the set is there, ready to edit and to export. Each synced deck becomes clips: a track from where you started it, and a new clip at every hot cue, beat jump, loop lap or new track, on the bar where it happened. Your fader, filter, delay and reverb sends, EQ, stem levels and mapped macros become curves; a control you never touched gets none. A channel the Composer cannot replay as a clip is recorded as audio while it sounds, and placed as an audio clip on its lane: a live input, the radio, an instrument played from the keys, or a deck playing without SYNC. Those takes land in Recordings with the other takes.

The set is saved as Capture with the date and time, in the sets folder. If nothing is playing when REC stops, it opens in the Composer at once, and the set you had open is saved first. If music is still playing, the capture only saves, so loading it cannot change the live tempo or fire clips into your decks; open it from COMPOSITIONS when the music stops. Not captured: a deck's key changes, and tempo changes (the set plays at the tempo the capture began at). Cadence says so when either happened.

Export audio

The COMPOSITIONS menu's Export audio… renders the set to files, the way a DAW exports an arrangement. The set plays as the Composer would play it, with every envelope, macro, stem move and plugin, and with the mixer as it stands. The render runs in the background, faster than real time, in a second, hidden Cadence. Your live set is not touched and can keep playing while the export runs.

  • Range: the whole set, from its first clip to the end of its last, then a few seconds more while the reverb and delay die away. Or the selected clip, the section you selected (Shift-drag the ruler), or any bars you type. A range is cut exactly at its last bar, with a short fade so it does not click. Two bars before the range play silently first, so the effects are already running when the file starts.
  • Format: WAV at 16 bit, 24 bit or 32-bit float; FLAC at 16 or 24 bit; or AAC (an .m4a at 256 kbps, made by the Mac's own encoder). The sample rate is 44.1, 48 or 96 kHz (AAC takes 44.1 or 48). A 16-bit file is dithered.
  • Files: the full mix, as the master output plays it. Tick Each channel as its own file for CH1 to CH4 as each reaches the master, after its fader and its plugins, plus an FX returns file with the shared reverb. The channel files and the FX returns add up to the mix before the master compressor and limiter, so you can remix the set elsewhere. Tick Tracklist for a .cue file that names each track and the moment it starts in the mix, in the Artist – Title form your Collection uses.
  • Loudness: every export measures the mix as streaming services do, in LUFS, with its true peak (the level between the samples that a converter or an encoder rebuilds). The summary shows both. Pick a target and the mix is brought to it: -14 LUFS for Spotify, YouTube and Tidal, -16 for Apple Music and podcasts, -11 for loud streaming masters, -9 for club and DJ mixes. The true peak always stays under -1 dBTP. Only the mix changes level, and no limiter is added: a mix too dynamic to reach a loud target lands quieter, and the summary says by how much.
  • Pre-master: tick it for a second mix taken before the master compressor and limiter, after the master fader. It is always a 32-bit float WAV, so nothing above 0 dB is cut, ready for a mastering engineer.
  • Where: a new folder named after the set and the minute, in ~/Music/Cadence Exports unless you choose another. The dialog remembers the format, the rate, the ticks and the folder.
  • While it runs: the dialog shows how far the render is. Cancel stops it and leaves nothing behind. Closing the window only hides it: the export carries on and Cadence tells you when it is done. At the end, Reveal in Finder opens the folder.
  • What it cannot hear: a live input, since the render has no audio device. Stem automation on a track without separated stems plays the plain track, and the dialog names those tracks. Separate them in the Collection first.

Sampler (samples & strips)

A non-destructive sample editor — sources are only read, never modified.

  • Open a track: press BROWSE (or C) to dock the Collection under the editor, then double-click a track — or drag one onto the waveform. Right-clicking a track anywhere in the COLLECTION → Open in Sampler works too.
  • Select: drag on the zoomable waveform, either way; SNAP locks edges to the beatgrid (a warped track’s own beats, and never past either end of the track); drag the edges to adjust. The readout shows seconds and beats. Wheel zooms by how far it moves, shift-wheel pans. A right-click keeps the selection. When a SCAN or the Track Editor changes the track’s grid, the Sampler takes the new grid at once and keeps the selection. A track with no grid greys SNAP and says why.
  • Save Sample — writes the region as a 24-bit WAV into ~/Music/Cadence/Sampler Out and adds it to the Collection’s Sampler Out (drum hits, vocals, stabs…). It is named after the track and where the region starts, for example Track - 1m23.4s.
  • Monitor it: PLAY plays the selection through the Sampler's own preview voice — instantly, straight from memory, with no deck involved. With nothing selected it plays the whole loaded track, so a file you just dragged in can be heard right away. LOOP repeats the region (the wrap is a hard splice, so you hear exactly what the saved sample will sound like), and a white playhead tracks the voice across the waveform.
  • Where it goes, and how loud: CUE and MAIN are two independent switches, not a choice of one — cue is your headphones, main is the room, and both can be on at once (or neither). CUE is on and MAIN is off by default, so pressing PLAY can never surprise a dance floor. VOL is the preview's own level; nothing on the mixer moves when you change it. Routing and level are remembered between sessions.
  • Keys and the menu: Space plays or stops, ⌘L loops, Esc clears the selection, ⌘A selects the whole track, ← → move the selection a beat (Shift: its end only), ⌘S saves the sample and ⌘D adds it to the strip. Shift-click extends the selection; a double-click selects the bar. Right-click the waveform for a menu with these commands and their keys, plus Select this bar, Select the section and the zoom. Every change to the strip — adding, removing, reordering, clearing — is one step of ⌘Z (⇧⌘Z redoes).
  • Saving and rendering run in the background, so the meters never freeze. A save or a render that fails says so and adds nothing to the Collection. A rendered strip is exactly as long as its parts: the 10 ms joins are centred on the boundaries, so every part starts on its own bar, and every part is converted to 44.1 kHz with a band-limited resampler. A part whose file is gone is left out and named in the message. RENDER offers a new name each time (Strip, Strip (2)…); a render saved over an older file replaces it in the Collection with its new length and waveform.
  • It is not a deck: the preview has no EQ, no filter, no FX and no channel fader, and it cannot take a deck away from you mid-set. (It replaces the old Audition CH4 button, which loaded a rendered temp file onto a deck — sometimes one that was live on the master.)
  • The STRIP: Add to Strip collects regions in order — from any number of tracks — and Render Strip bounces them into one joint clip (44.1k/24-bit, equal-power crossfades at every join). Your quick re-edit/mashup workflow.
  • Where they land: saved samples and rendered strips go to ~/Music/Cadence/Sampler Out and into the collection, tagged Sampler Out; the docked Collection below picks them up straight away.
  • The pads: eight pads sit under the waveform. Right-click a pad and choose Put the selection here (or the whole track), or drop a file, a Collection track or the last saved sample on it. Press a pad to play it, or press 1–8 while the Sampler is open. The pad’s menu sets how it plays: One-shot plays once, Loop plays until you press it again, Gate plays while you hold it. It also sets when it starts: at once, on the next beat or on the next bar of the master. A pad waiting for its beat pulses; a playing pad lights and shows how far it has played. Pads play to the room by default and are part of the mix, so the master fader, the limiter and a recording include them; Out → Headphones keeps a pad in your phones to check it first. Every pad can be mapped in MAP mode, and a gate pad mapped to a controller pad plays while you hold it. The pads and their settings are saved with your session.
  • Sample from the stems: when a track has separated stems, STEMS chooses which of them the Sampler takes: the vocals, the drums, everything but the bass, any mix of the four. PLAY, SAVE SAMPLE, ADD TO STRIP and the pads then use only those stems, and a saved sample is named after them (“Track - 1m02.0s (vocals)”). The whole track goes back to the file itself. A track without stems greys the button: separate its stems in the Collection first.
  • Samples keep the grid: a sample saved from a track with a beatgrid takes that grid with it: its tempo, its first bar line, and the track’s key. It loads onto a deck in sync, without a scan. A strip whose parts share one tempo keeps it too. Right-click SNAP to snap to a bar, a beat, or a half, quarter, eighth or sixteenth of a beat; the arrows then move the selection by that step. SYNC makes PLAY wait for the master’s next bar and play at the master’s tempo, with the pitch following like a deck without key lock, so a loop lands in time with the mix. Right-click the waveform to slice the selection: four or eight equal slices, or one a beat, onto the pads (from pad 1) or into the strip.
  • The strip as a lane: the strip’s parts show as blocks above the pads, in the order they render, each as wide as it is long and tinted by its track. Drag a block to move it, click it to open its region in the Sampler again, right-click it to move or remove it. PLAY STRIP plays the whole strip, joins and all, before you render it; a line on the lane shows where.

Plugins, chains & macros

Chains

Every channel hosts up to three plugins in series (AU/VST3 effects or instruments). Click the strip's FX chip:

  • Loaded slots get submenus — open editor, bypass, remove, pin to favorites.
  • Favorites (your pinned list, then most-used) and the full manufacturer-grouped list load into the first empty slot; the editor pops open ready to tweak, floating above Cadence even fullscreen.
  • Plugin manager / scan… opens the manager: scan for plugins, double-click favorites onto an exact channel + slot (CH and S1–S3 selectors).

Chains, plugin settings, bypass states and macro assignments all persist — quit and relaunch, and your rig rebuilds itself.

Macros

See Master section — M1–M8 drive Cadence controls and plugin parameters, several per macro with ranges and invert, by learn, menu or the editor; they are MIDI-learnable and controllable from the remote. Every hosted plugin editor carries a header strip with TO MACRO, BYPASS and REMOVE. A removed plugin takes its targets off the macros; a reordered chain carries them along.

TO MACRO. Touch a control in the plugin's editor, and the button names it. Click it for two choices. New macro makes the first empty macro the kind you pick: a slider to sweep the parameter, an on/off (on is the top of the range, off the bottom) or a radio of two to eight steps. The new macro's editor then opens beside the button, to set the range, invert it or name it. The parameter stays where it is until you first use the new macro; a new on/off reads ON when the parameter sits nearer the top. Add to lists your macros with their kind and how many controls each drives, ticked where the parameter already is.

The macro under its plugin. In Perform, a macro that drives a plugin shows under that plugin in the channel strip, as the same control it has in the MACROS band. A slider is a fader. An on/off reads ON or OFF and switches with a click. A radio is a row of its steps. Right-click any of them for the macro's menu. In MAP, each one binds the same target as the macro's control in the MACROS band.

Idle DSP: loaded chains process every block, like DAW tracks — an idling synth costs real CPU. Bypass or remove chains you're not using (the FX chip menu) to reclaim it. The engine readout shows the average load.

Latency compensation

Some plugins look ahead, such as a limiter or a linear-phase EQ. They report how late their sound is. The latency of a channel is the sum of its enabled plugins. Cadence delays every other clip channel until the slowest one is ready, so all of them reach the master and the cue together. Their delay and reverb sends wait with them. The metronome, MIDI clock out and the OSC beat move by the same amount, so drum machines and visuals stay on the beat you hear. A take is placed where you played it against what you heard.

Live playing is never delayed. A channel on its live input is exempt. So is a channel whose instrument you play from a keyboard or a Launchpad: it plays at once and does not make the others wait. It counts as played for 8 seconds after your last note. Notes from a MIDI clip, the Composer, a sequencer plugin or a track's MIDI layer do not count, so that channel waits like a clip channel.

A long chain runs ahead instead. A clip deck whose plugins add 20 ms or more (Sway’s 160 ms lookahead, say) does not make the others wait: the deck runs that far ahead of the room, so its sound still lands on the beat, and nothing else moves, not the other channels and not the clock. Its launches, loops, cues and stem edits are taken that much early and heard on the bar. The one thing you hear is on that deck alone: when such a plugin lands on a playing deck, the deck dips for about the plugin’s latency (it fades out, jumps ahead, waits for the plugin to fill, fades in); when it leaves, the deck dips for a few milliseconds. A plugin mounted on a stopped deck costs nothing. The deck’s playhead is drawn where the room hears it.

Mono and sidechain plugins. Cadence feeds every plugin the channel’s stereo pair. A plugin that only takes a mono input hears the pair folded to one and its output goes to both sides; a plugin with a sidechain input it cannot switch off gets silence on it; a plugin with one output feeds both sides. So an insert built for a mono track, or a compressor with an external sidechain, works on a channel without a setting.

The wait is at most one second. The controls of a waiting channel act before its wait, so its EQ and fader answer that much later. Keep look-ahead plugins for the channels that need them. The Latency row of the system check says how much the plugins add and on which channel.

MIDI routing & VST jamming

MIDI flows through each chain like a DAW track: slot 1's MIDI output feeds slot 2, and so on. That means a sequencer plugin in slot 1 (Tonnetz, Metro 185) plays an instrument in slot 2 (Cell) on the same channel — locked to the master clock via the host playhead. A plugin that makes no MIDI of its own, such as an audio effect (Glue) or a synth, passes the notes on unchanged, so an instrument plays whatever slot it is in: Glue in slot 1 and Cell in slot 2 works as well as the other way round. Plugins after the fader get no notes. A sequencer leaves the channel's sound alone: drop Metro 185 or Tonnetz on a channel playing a clip and the clip keeps playing while the sequencer's notes go on down the chain. Many sequencer and arpeggiator plugins silence the audio they are handed, so Cadence checks each plugin that sends MIDI as it loads, and the channel's audio goes around one that would silence it. A plugin that sends MIDI and also changes the sound, such as Contour, stays in the audio path.

  • Cross-channel: the FX menu's MIDI out → CH n routes a chain's MIDI to another channel — an empty deck becomes a pure MIDI-send device driving an instrument elsewhere.
  • Key-follow: SHLabs modules with a FOLLOW switch (Tonnetz) listen to Cadence's module bus and lock their key/scale to whatever track currently leads the mix — the reference hands over automatically as the next song takes over. VST jams stay in key with the set.
  • Hardware/performance MIDI (a keyboard) feeds every chain alongside the routed events.

Remote control

Cadence serves its own browser remote on your local network — phone or tablet, no app. Press H and scan the "remote control" QR (or type the address shown).

  • Master: play/stop, BPM, beat dots.
  • Per deck: track name, PLAY, LOOP, position bar, a big fader, FADE IN/OUT (gradual 8-beat ramps) and MIX 32 / MIX 8 — auto-transition into that deck from the current lead, slow or fast.
  • Macros: all eight, as sliders.
  • Everything is quantized — remote actions land on the next bar while the clock runs.

Choose what the remote exposes. In SETTINGS → REMOTE, Features… ticks six groups on or off: tempo, Assist, decks, the collection and Next-Up queue, radio and macros. Everything is on unless you turn it off. The choice is enforced by the server, not just hidden on the page — a switched-off feature is refused whoever asks — and it takes effect at once, without restarting the server or invalidating the link on a phone already paired.

Let the room ask for a track. Guest link in the same group opens a second QR and copies its address. Anyone who scans it can browse your collection and request a track; they cannot touch the mix, the tempo or the decks, and every other route is refused for a guest link. Requests are capped, deduped, and rate-limited per device, and they land in their own list — not in Next-Up. You accept or dismiss them under Assist → Guest requests on the desktop, or on the REQUESTS card of your own remote page. Accepting one adds it to Next-Up exactly as a right-click would. The guest link needs LAN access on, since a link to 127.0.0.1 is one only this Mac can open.

The page lays itself out for the screen it is on: one column on a phone, two from about 700 px, three from 1000 px, with bigger hit targets and taller deck overviews on a tablet. Add &layout=phone or &layout=tablet to the address to pin a shape.

Requirements: same Wi-Fi network (no guest/isolated VLAN); allow Cadence in the macOS firewall if prompted. Toggle the remote on/off by clicking the engine readout. Prefer a dedicated OSC app? Cadence's OSC-in mapping works with TouchOSC and friends too.

OSC & the module bus

Main OSC out

The OSC panel points a configurable target (host/port) at visualizers or show control — SHLabs Phosphor locks its visuals to it. Messages include /cadence/bpm, /cadence/beat, /cadence/phase, /cadence/transport, per-channel and master levels, band energies, kick onsets, a section-intensity signal, and the live section's index/type and progress-through.

Stem bus

When a deck is playing separated stems, Cadence also broadcasts what the mix is made of: /cadence/stems/drums, /bass, /other and /cadence/stems/vocals (0..1 levels of the audible mix, taken after the per-stem gains and mutes, so killing the drums takes them out of the feed) plus /cadence/stems/drums/onset, a per-hit spike from the drum stem alone that no bassline can trigger. Several stem decks are combined by taking the loudest, not by adding. /cadence/stems/active (0/1) says whether any audible deck is on stems — it repeats about twice a second so a module can join mid-set; the levels are sent only while it is 1.

Module bus

A second, always-on local feed (127.0.0.1:9009) carries clock, transport and /cadence/key — the live set's current key, including any key shift. SHLabs modules hosted on the same machine (Tonnetz's FOLLOW) use it without any setup, and without touching your main OSC target. Toggle it in the engine-options menu (click the DSP readout).

OSC in

Incoming OSC can be mapped generically to controls — see the OSC panel. A learned address carries a value from 0 to 1, like a MIDI fader.

The master tempo by OSC. /cadence/bpm/set with one number sets the master tempo in BPM, for example /cadence/bpm/set 128.5. It needs no learning, and a value outside 60 to 180 is held at the nearest end. Cadence sends the tempo out on /cadence/bpm, so a sender can read and set it the same way. While Cadence follows a MIDI clock, the clock sets the tempo and this address is ignored.

MAP: MIDI, macros and OSC

Press MAP in the top bar, in Perform or the Composer. The window dims, every mappable control is outlined, and the banner holds three switches. MIDI: click a control on screen, then move a MIDI control to bind it; right-click clears its MIDI binding. MACRO: pick a macro, then click controls (or move a mapped control or a plugin knob) to add them to it, with the macro's editor beside you (see Macros). OSC: click a control, then send it an OSC message to bind that address; right-click clears it, and the banner says which port Cadence listens on. DONE or Esc ends the session. In the Composer the tools and draw targets and each lane's fader are mappable too; a lane's fader is its channel's volume, so one binding drives both. Faders, EQs, filters, cue/on buttons, the master tempo, deck play, sync, loops, hot cues, the eight macros, the stem chips, every clip-grid slot and each deck's jog are mappable. So are the stem moves, a solo, an echo out and three rolls per stem, and slip and pitch play: each deck's SLIP, its eight pitch pads and its pitch-play cue. The stem moves and the pitch pads have no control of their own on screen, so learn them from the mapping list. Mappings persist.

Map the master tempo. In MAP, click the BPM field in the top bar, then move a control. A fader or a knob sets the tempo across 60 to 180 BPM; narrow its range in the binding to ride a smaller window, for example 120 to 132 BPM across the whole knob. An endless encoder (turn it one way, then the other, while Learn listens) moves the tempo a step at a time. Pads can step it: Master Tempo +1, -1, +0.1 and -0.1 are in the mapping list. A macro can drive it too: a slider sweeps its range, an on/off jumps between two tempos, a radio picks one of its steps, and the macro's editor says the range in BPM. While Cadence follows a MIDI clock, the clock sets the tempo and these do nothing.

The full list of bindings, the input device picker and your saved map profiles live in SETTINGS → MIDI mapping….

Controller feel

Every binding has a MODE button in the mapping list. It sets how the control's messages become a value, and it is set once per binding:

  • Absolute — the control's position is the value. The default, and what every existing mapping keeps.
  • Soft takeover — a fader that is not where the software fader is does nothing until it crosses it, then follows. While it waits, an amber badge sits on the mapped control pointing the way to move, and the row says catch: raise or catch: lower.
  • Relative — for endless encoders, which send a step rather than a position. Three conventions are in the wild (2's complement, signed bit, bin offset); pick one, or let Learn find it. Encoder step switches between a full sweep in 127 ticks and a tenth of that.
  • Toggle — for a pad that sends note-on and never a release. Each press flips, instead of latching down for the rest of the set.

The same menu carries Invert and a Range, a window of the target the control reaches — a fader that only ever moves the filter between 10% and 90%, for instance.

Learn finds the mode for you. After it binds, keep moving the control for a moment: turn an endless encoder one way then the other and Learn recognises it and its convention; a 14-bit CC pair announces itself the same way; a pad that never sends a release is proposed as a toggle. The binding stays silent for that moment, so an encoder's 127 can never land as a position.

Feedback out (LEDs and motor faders)

Pick a MIDI Output in the mapping dialog and switch on Send feedback, and every mapped value goes back to the controller in the message type it listens on — a CC value for a knob, a note velocity for a pad LED. It follows the value from any source: the mouse, automation, Assist, or another binding.

It is off until you turn it on, per device, because some controllers echo what they receive. A value that arrived from the controller is never sent straight back to it, which is the other half of keeping that loop shut. Feedback is coalesced and rate-limited to one message per control per 20 ms, on the message thread — the audio thread is never involved.

Controllers

A controller drives Cadence through a MIDI map. Cadence ships a profile for each controller it knows, and keeps yours beside them.

Profiles

SETTINGS → MIDI mapping… → Profile lists the factory profiles, your own profiles, Save as profile… and Reveal profiles folder. A factory profile whose controller is plugged in says (connected). Loading a profile replaces your bindings, and Cadence asks first when you have any. It never changes the MIDI input and output you chose in the two boxes above.

A loaded profile is your map from then on: change it, and the change is saved as any change is. The factory copy never changes. Save as profile… keeps the current map under a name of your own.

When a controller Cadence has a profile for is plugged in and nothing is mapped, the dot after CONTROL SURFACES in Settings turns amber, and the Controller row of the system check offers Load profile. One click maps the controller and listens to it. Nothing loads on its own.

M-Audio Xsession Pro

The first factory profile. The four faders, left to right, are the volumes of decks 1 to 4. The six knobs on the A side are deck 1's filter, delay and reverb and its three EQ bands; the B side does the same for deck 2. The headphone buttons are the cue of decks 1 and 2, and the crossfader is the headphone level. On each side, rewind and fast forward jump back and forward by the quantize grid, play/pause plays, and cue jumps to the deck's first cue pad: the track's earliest hot cue. The Xsession Pro has no jog wheels and no browse or load controls.

Jog wheels

Bind CH n Jog to a jog wheel or an endless encoder: press Learn, then turn it one way and then the other.

  • Playing, the jog nudges. The deck speeds up or slows down with the wheel, by up to 8 %, and is back at its own speed about a fifth of a second after you stop. The shift it made stays, with SYNC on or off: this is how you fix a flam by ear. With KEY LOCK on a synced deck the pitch holds; otherwise it bends for a moment, like a record under a finger.
  • Stopped, the jog moves where Play will start, an eighth of a beat a tick, silently. The overview and the near view follow. Play then starts on the grid as always, from the beat nearest that point that keeps the bar in phase.

A binding set to Fine step moves a tenth as far. A deck on the live input or radio, or playing a MIDI clip, ignores the jog.

Browse and load

Browse Scroll moves the Collection's selected row, one row a tick, and stops at the first and the last row. CH n Load Selected loads the selected track onto deck n, exactly as the Collection's LOAD buttons do. With nothing selected it does nothing.

Recording

REC records the master bus (post compressor and limiter) as a 24-bit WAV into ~/Music/Cadence Recordings. With Also save REC as a set on in Settings, it also captures the set into the Composer (see Capture a live set). Press again to stop. The recording is listed in the Collection under Recordings, with every take. With an external mixer there is no master bus, so record the mixer’s output instead.

The plugin chain's order

Load a plugin while the music plays. A plugin loaded into a playing channel fades in over a few milliseconds instead of switching in, and Cadence runs it a few times on its own before it reaches the audio, so its first moments cost nothing live. A plugin that delays its sound (a lookahead limiter, a linear-phase EQ) keeps the channel's dry sound until its own sound arrives, then crossfades to it, at the moment the other channels move to stay in time with it. Saving the session, which happens on its own after a change, no longer interrupts any plugin.

A channel's plugins run in the order the PLUGINS section shows them, top to bottom: the number on each chip is its place in the signal, 1 first. Pre-fader plugins run before the EQ, the filter and the fader; plugins under the FADER line run after the fader. The order matters: two effects sound different swapped, a MIDI sequencer has to sit before the synth it plays, and an effect placed before an instrument never touches that instrument's sound (a + on the spine marks where an instrument's sound joins the channel). Drag a chip up or down to reorder. Drop it under the FADER line to make it post-fader, above to make it pre-fader; an instrument stays above the line, since below it it would get no notes. The move lands on the channel's declick dip, so nothing clicks. Right-click a chip for Move up and Move down (the same moves without a drag), and Post-fader, which moves the plugin straight to the line. A plugin that delays the signal shows its latency at the right of its chip on a wide strip, or a small tick on a narrow one; compensation covers it either way. A move is refused while a plugin is still loading on that channel or while the channel records a take.

Takes on a channel

The small REC chip in a deck's header records a take into that channel: press it, pick the loop length and the count-in, and play. The take starts on the bar line after the count-in and loops when its bars are up. While it counts and records, the chip's letter says which layer is filling (M for MIDI, A for audio) and the deck's clock row reads COUNT-IN or RECORDING with the same word. A channel on its live input offers an AUDIO take (what the input hears) or a MIDI take (what you play on your keyboard); a channel playing a track offers a MIDI take. A MIDI take also records what a plugin on the channel plays: a MIDI generator such as Tonnetz, an arpeggiator, or a quantizer that moves your notes to the scale. The take holds the notes as that plugin hands them on, so a quantized note replaces the one you pressed, and a synth after it that plays the notes changes nothing. The notes of a clip already playing on the channel are not recorded again. A MIDI take needs no instrument on the channel. The notes land on the channel's MIDI layer and loop in the phase you played them; mount a synth or a MIDI effect later and it plays them. A channel with nothing to play MIDI yet says so when the take lands, and an empty clip deck takes a MIDI take too. Radio channels do not record takes. In the REC menu's INPUT row, click the lit input again to switch the channel's input off.

A deck's MIDI layer is part of the session: quit and relaunch, and the take is back on its deck, parked, ready for PLAY. Every MIDI take is also written as a .mid file into the takes folder, so it is listed under Recordings and can be dragged into a DAW; the file remembers the loop length, so reloading it loops over the bars you recorded. SETTINGS → LIBRARY → Keep MIDI takes as .mid files switches the file off; the take still lands on the deck. A deck playing a track keeps its LOOP buttons on the track when a MIDI take lands beside it; the take's own loop is re-cut from the Composer's lane. On a deck holding a take, the loop cluster becomes TAKE LOOP: /2 halves the loop and x2 doubles it. A loop longer than the recording plays the recording again inside it (the cell reads TAKE 8B x2); nothing changes until you overdub a variation on a later lap, which is what the doubling is for.

Engine options

System check

The system check tells you whether your setup is ready to make music. It lives in SETTINGS, beside the settings it judges. It looks at twelve things: the audio device, cue routing (the deck outputs, with an external mixer), latency, dropouts since launch, CPU load, whether your tracks are on disk and scanned, cached stems, the controller, the phone remote, free disk space for a recording, and the MIDI clock (the one Cadence follows, and its clock out). Each gets a green, amber or red verdict. Amber means Cadence runs, but something is worse than it could be. Red means something you set up is not working.

The dots. A small dot sits after each Settings heading that has a check behind it: AUDIO, LIBRARY, STEMS, CONTROL SURFACES, REMOTE and CLOCK & SYNC. The dot shows the worst verdict in that group. Hover it to read each check and what to do about it. Click it to open the check page. The dots update when Settings opens and a moment after you change a setting.

The check page. Press SYSTEM CHECK at the top right of Settings, or System check under HELP. The settings body swaps for one row per check, with a summary line at the top (All good, or how many rows need a look). Where the fix is a single control, the control sits in the row and you change it there: the cue output (or the deck-outputs layout, with an external mixer), the buffer size (for latency, dropouts and CPU load), the remote's On and LAN switches, and the MIDI clock port. Scan scans the tracks that have no beatgrid, and Separate separates stems for as many tracks as the stems cap is short of. With Separate tracks as they load on, the stems row stays green, since a track separates when it loads, and it says how many tracks are separating on the decks right now. When some of your stems came from an older separation of a file that is not 44.1 kHz, the stems row stays green and offers Re-separate: it separates exactly those tracks again, which gives their OTHER stem a cleaner top end. Where the fix needs a whole group, such as picking an audio device, downloading the stems model or changing the remote's port, the row's button takes you back to the settings page and outlines that group for a second. Mapping and Collection open their own windows. When a controller Cadence has a profile for is plugged in and nothing is mapped, the controller row turns amber and Load profile maps it in one click. The check runs again after each change you make from the page, and Check again runs it on demand.

Latency turns amber above 12 ms of output latency. Dropouts turn amber at ten audio overloads since launch; a few while loading tracks or plugins are normal. CPU load is the audio engine's share of its real-time budget: amber from 70%, red from 90%.

Updates & diagnostics

The update notice. Cadence tells you when a newer build is out. It never installs anything and never interrupts a set: no pop-up, no toast. The news is one line in SETTINGS under HELP, beside the Check for updates switch, and the same line in the About box. It reads Up to date, or Cadence 0.7.1 is out. Get it. Hover that line for a one-line note on what changed, and click it to open the download page on shlabs.ch. From the next launch, until you open SETTINGS once for that version, a small dot sits in the corner of the SETTINGS button. The dot never appears in the middle of a session.

What is fetched, and what is sent. About two minutes after launch, and at most once a day, Cadence downloads one small file, https://shlabs.ch/cadence/latest.json. It names the newest version for the Mac and for Windows. The request sends nothing about you or your music: no account, no identifier, no settings, no track names. Like any web request, it reaches the server with your internet address. When you are offline, nothing happens, and Cadence asks again the next day. To stop it, turn off Check for updates in SETTINGS → HELP.

Installing is still up to you: download the new build and replace the old app. Updates that install themselves come once Cadence is notarized.

Diagnostics file

When something goes wrong, one file tells SHLabs what your setup was. Click the CADENCE wordmark (top right) and press Save diagnostics file. After a crash, the next launch offers the same button. Cadence asks where to save it (the Desktop, unless you choose another folder) and writes one zip, Cadence-diagnostics-date-time.zip. Then Email SHLabs opens a mail to shlabs.contact@gmail.com with the version in the subject, and shows you the file so you can attach it.

What is inside. summary.txt holds the Cadence version and build, the system, the processor and memory, the audio device with its sample rate, buffer and latency, the routing, your MIDI devices and whether a map is loaded, each channel's plugins with their versions and latency, the stems model, and the settings that change how Cadence behaves. It names no track. Beside it are cadence.log and the older cadence.log.1, which do list the tracks you loaded, and on the Mac the crash report macOS wrote after the last crash. In all of these files your home folder is written as ~. Nothing is sent: the file stays where you saved it until you attach it yourself.

Troubleshooting

Why is this greyed? Hover it. Every control Cadence disables carries a tooltip that says the reason and what to do about it — that is the rule, not the exception, and it is the fastest answer to anything in this list.

  • The remote page won't load — phone and Mac must be on the same Wi-Fi (guest networks often isolate clients); allow Cadence in System Settings → Network → Firewall. The QR always encodes the current working address.
  • High DSP while idle — loaded plugin chains run like DAW tracks. Bypass or remove unused plugins via the FX chip.
  • Loops/cues don't snap — the track has no beatgrid yet: scan it, or set the grid by eye in the beatgrid editor.
  • Wrong tempo (half/double) — use /2 or x2 next to the BPM field in the collection.
  • SYNC'd track sounds pitched — KEY (key-lock) is off on that deck; light it up.
  • Key column empty — keys come from scanning (or rekordbox import).
  • A deck won't launch — the master clock must be running; launches are quantized, so they wait for the grid point set by Quantize in SETTINGS.
  • Audio device vanished — the red banner means the interface disconnected; Cadence reconnects automatically when it returns.
  • No STEMS row on the deck: that track has no stems, and it is not separating. Install the model in SETTINGS, and keep Separate tracks as they load on, or right-click the track in the COLLECTION → Separate stems.
  • Stems won't turn on: you are at the deck cap; turn them off on another deck, or raise SETTINGS → Stems → Stems on up to. A greyed STEMS row is a deck waiting for a free stems deck.
  • The mixer disappeared — the console header cycles full → levels → hidden. Click MIXER - HIDDEN at the top of the deck area to bring it back.
