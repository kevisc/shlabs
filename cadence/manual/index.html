Cadence Manual

The SHLabs hybrid performance mixer — four channels of decks, live inputs, plugins and automation, locked to one clock.

Welcome

Cadence is a performance mixer built around one idea: everything follows one clock. Four channels each play either a live input (hardware, synths, a eurorack mix) or an internal deck (a track from your collection). Decks beat-match to the master tempo, launches and loops land on the grid, plugins follow the host clock, and partner instruments can even follow the key of the playing track — so backing tracks, loops, hardware and VST instruments all stay in time and in tune while you mix.

  • Perform — the mixer: four strips, master section, track overviews.
  • Collection — your library: scanning, search, playlists, key-compatible browsing.
  • Composer — a timeline: lay out a set in advance with automation envelopes.
  • Sampler — a sample editor: snip sounds out of tracks, render strip re-edits.

Quick start

  • 1. Add music. Go to COLLECTION, click Add Files (or Import XML for a rekordbox collection).
  • 2. Scan. Select tracks and press Scan. This detects BPM, the beatgrid with its bar downbeat, the musical key, and the loudness (for auto-gain). Several tracks scan in parallel.
  • 3. Load. Use the LOAD 1–4 buttons (they glow with the live level of each channel so you don't load over a playing deck), or right-click → Load playing (fader down) to audition.
  • 4. Play. Back in PERFORM, press a deck's PLAY (or keys Q W E R). The button blinks while armed and fires on the next quantize point. A deck starts the master clock itself, so there is nothing to press first — the master PLAY lives in COMPOSER, for running the timeline.
  • 5. Mix. SYNC keeps decks locked to the master tempo (with key-lock, so pitch doesn't bend). Ride the faders and EQs, drop loops, and watch the track overview for the red end-of-track warning.
Tip: press H any time (or Keyboard shortcuts… in SETTINGS) for the shortcut overlay — it also shows the QR codes for the remote control and this manual. Any key closes it.

The interface

Mode tabs (top left)

PERFORM COMPOSER COLLECTION SAMPLER switch the whole surface (keys 1–4, left to right). The transport bar stays on top in every mode.

Transport bar

PLAY starts the master clock (and sends MIDI Start). The BPM slider (60–180) eases to a new tempo gradually instead of snapping. T is tap-tempo, M the metronome (right-click it to route the click to master and/or headphones and set its level). LINK joins an Ableton Link session. Quantize — the one grid for everything — lives in SETTINGS; see Transport & quantize.

Top bar buttons

Left to right: BROWSE (dock the collection into PERFORM, COMPOSER or SAMPLER, also C), ASSIST (auto-mixing — right-click for its options) and REC. Then, after a gap, the configuration group: FX (delay & reverb), COMPRESSOR, OSC, MIDI (mapping mode — PERFORM only) and SETTINGS. Beside them sit the engine readout (buffer · output latency · DSP load — click it for engine options) and the CADENCE wordmark (click for About & licenses).

The top bar stays deliberately short: the plugin manager, the MIDI mapping list, UI zoom, the waveform colour choice and the shortcut overlay all live inside SETTINGS.

Channel strips

Each of the four strips is one channel. The beat dots in its header show the deck's bar phase against the master grid.

Player panel

  • Source — toggles between INPUT (the audio interface input for this channel) and PLAYER (the internal deck).
  • Clip name — click to load a file; right-click to eject the track (your cue/loop edits are saved back to the collection first). You can also drag an audio file straight onto the strip.
  • PLAY — quantized launch: blinks while armed, starts exactly on the grid. Press again to stop (also quantized). De-clicked fades on start/stop.
  • LOOP — whether the clip loops at its end or plays one-shot.
  • SYNC — locks the deck's beatgrid to the master clock (beat-matched, phase-snapped). On by default.
  • →BPM — glides the master tempo gradually to this track's original BPM (ease the whole set back to native speed, unnoticed).
  • KEY — key-lock and key shifting; see Key-lock.
  • FX chip — shows the plugin chain ("EQ > Comp"); click it for the plugin menu. See Plugins.

Perform panel

  • Hot cues 1–4 — click to set, click again to jump (quantized), shift-click to clear. Stored with the track.
  • < > — beat-jump by the quantize size (shift-click = 4×).
  • /2 LOOP 4 x2 — the auto-loop: loop N beats from the current beat; halve/double the length live. The button pulses while looping.
  • 1/4 1/2 1 2 — loop rolls: hold to stutter, release to snap back in time. See Loops & rolls.

Sound shaping

Three-band EQ (HIGH/MID/LOW, full kill at the bottom), a dual-slope FILTER (low-pass left of centre, high-pass right), and DLY/VERB sends into the built-in tempo delay and reverb (their character is set in the FX panel). The FX+/FX− VOL+/VOL− FLT+/FLT− chips run gradual automation ramps over bars — hands-free build-ups and fade-outs.

Fader, trim and routing

The channel fader tops out at 0 dB — headroom lives on the master only. Under the hood every scanned track also carries an auto-gain trim toward −14 LUFS, so decks come up at comparable loudness. CUE sends the channel pre-fader to your headphones; ON assigns it to the master bus (quantized mute/unmute).

Centre console & MACROS

The mixer console sits between the deck columns. Its header cycles through three sizes on each click, so you can trade console for deck space mid-set:

  • MIXER — the full channel strips.
  • MIXER - LEVELS — bare level strips: meter, fader and PLUGINS only.
  • MIXER - HIDDEN — no console at all; the decks take the whole width.

The header row itself always stays, so the console can never get lost. Drag either edge to trade width with the decks — the full and compact sizes remember their own widths.

MACROS is now its own collapsible band at the bottom of the console (click the header to open or close it): eight horizontal bars in two columns — M1–M4 down the left, M5–M8 down the right — each captioned with the parameter it drives, plus a page navigator when you have more than one page and the same right-click assignment as before. It hides with the console. See Master section.

Master section & macros

  • Master fader — the only fader allowed above 0 dB. Stereo meters beside it.
  • LIMIT — burns red while the brick-wall safety limiter is actually reducing gain. If you see it often, pull the master down.
  • COMPRESSOR — the master glue compressor: threshold, ratio, soft knee, attack/release, makeup, and a sidechain high-pass so the kick stops pumping the mix. Live gain-reduction meter with 3/6/12 dB marks.
  • MACROS M1–M8 — eight bars, each bound to any parameter of any loaded plugin. Two ways to bind: right-click a macro → Learn: touch a control in a plugin editor, then wiggle the control you want in the plugin window; or, in the plugin editor, touch a control and press TO MACRO in its header to send that parameter to the macro you pick. Macros are MIDI-learnable like any control, so a hardware knob can drive any plugin parameter. Assignments persist.
  • CUE/MIX & PHONES — headphone blend between the cue bus and the master, and the headphone level.

TRACKS band

Across the top of PERFORM, TRACKS shows the full-length waveform of every loaded deck side by side — always frequency-coloured (bass warm, mids green, highs blue), so you read each track's shape and energy at a glance while deciding what comes next.

Only decks with something in them get a lane (a track, a radio stream, or a MIDI instrument), so the band is as tall as it has things to show: it grows as you load and shrinks as you eject, and with every deck empty it steps aside to leave just its header.

  • Click a lane to seek that deck (quantized). Clicking a stopped deck starts it from that point.
  • Drag the divider below the band to make the lanes taller or shorter.
  • Click the header to collapse the band to a single row and give the height back to the decks.

Cues, loops and section editing stay in the deck overviews — this band is for seeing and seeking.

Track overviews & WAVEFORM

Each deck carries its own overview of the loaded track:

  • Click anywhere to seek there (quantized) and play. Each lane is tagged CH n with a live level meter so strips and lanes stay linked.
  • Sections from the section editor show as coloured, labelled bands; hot cues as amber markers; the loop region in azure with draggable IN/OUT handles.
  • End-of-track warning — the remaining tail pulses red inside 30 seconds and shows a countdown inside 15 (quiet while a loop is engaged).
  • Waveform colour — in SETTINGS: Song sections (the section colours speak) or Frequency tint (bass warm, mids green, highs blue).
  • A ghost playhead appears during a loop roll — the real position running underneath the stutter.

WAVEFORM

The WAVEFORM band at the very bottom is a collapsible section header — click it to open or close. It scrolls the played audio of every deck against the master grid, with a zoom control, so you can see at a glance whether two decks' transients line up. Its scope panel is titled PHASE. Display only: click-seeking and loop-dragging live in the deck overviews above.

Deck sources & radio

Each deck's SRC button shows what it is playing: IN (live input), CLIP (the deck player), GRID (the clip grid) or RAD (an internet radio stream). Left-click flips between live input and the player; right-click opens the full source menu:

  • LIVE INPUT — the audio interface input for this channel.
  • CLIP PLAYER — the ordinary deck face.
  • CLIP GRID — the 8-slot pad face; see Clip grid.
  • RADIO ▸ — a submenu listing your saved stations by name (tick = playing here), plus Browse stations… to find and save more.

CLIP PLAYER and CLIP GRID are two faces of one source, so switching between them never disturbs playback, and each deck remembers the face you left it on. Leaving RADIO stops the stream first.

Clip grid

Every deck can wear a grid of 8 clip slots instead of its zoomed waveform: right-click SRC → CLIP GRID. The slim full-track overview underneath stays live for seeking and loops. The grid is a chooser for the deck's one player, not a second voice — so a launch is the usual quantized launch.

  • Fill a slot — drag a track onto it, or highlight a track in COLLECTION and tap an empty pad (that only files it, nothing launches, so a mis-tap can't interrupt the deck).
  • Tap a filled pad to launch it on the next quantize point; tap again to stop. Tapping during the load cancels the queued launch.
  • Right-click a pad for Assign selected library track, Loop on/off (per slot, kept through a reassign), Clear slot and Assign from file….
  • Pads outline when they hold the deck's clip, blink while armed and pulse on the beat while playing.
  • MIDI-learnable — every slot on every deck appears in the mapper as CH1 Grid Slot 1 … so a pad controller can fire them.

Grids are saved with the session. If a clip's file has moved or been deleted since you last played, the slot is cleared on load (the session reports how many items it couldn't restore), and tapping a pad whose file has vanished warns you without stopping the deck that's playing.

Stems

Cadence can split a track into drums, bass, other and vocals and play it back from those four parts — drop the vocal, keep the drums under the next tune, solo a bassline.

  • One-time setup — open SETTINGS and press Download model (316 MB). It fetches once and stays.
  • Separate a track — in COLLECTION, right-click it → Separate stems (several selected tracks queue up). Results are cached, so a track is only ever separated once. Do it before the gig, not during.
  • Play them — a loaded track that has stems grows a STEMS row on its deck: the STEMS toggle plus four chips, DRM BSS OTH VOC. Lit = audible. Click a chip to mute it, right-click to solo (right-click again to release the solo). Stem edits are quantized: a chip click, a solo and the STEMS toggle itself all land on the next QUANT boundary and blink until they do, so a kill drops on the bar and not half a beat late — shift-click for instant, and a second click on a blinking chip takes it back out of the queue.
  • Read the STEMS button — it fills solid the moment you ask for stems, not when they finish loading, and the small square in its corner says where the deck actually is: hollow = off, blinking = the stems are still being read from disk, solid = they are playing. Off, the button is an outline; on, it is a filled block, so which state a deck is in reads across the booth.
  • Set the chips up before you switch stems on — on a track that has stems, clicking a chip with STEMS off switches stems on for that deck and records the mute, which lands with the stems the moment they are ready. While stems are off the chips are drawn quietly: the mute state is still readable, and the absence of the lit fill is what tells you nothing is affecting the audio yet. If the deck cap refuses, the click does nothing at all beyond the toast that says so. The mute set survives switching stems off and on — kill the vocal, drop stems for a bar, bring them back and the vocal is still killed. Loading another track or ejecting clears it, and it is never written to disk, so a fresh launch always starts all-audible.
  • Let Assist use them — when Stem transitions is on (the ASSIST features menu, on by default), the auto-DJ mixes with the parts instead of only the EQ: a bass swap trades the actual basslines on the swap bar, the incoming vocal is held back until the outgoing track is out of the way, a build-up strips the outgoing tune down to drums and bass under the riser, and when both tracks are separated it can hand over drums first, then bass — the way you would. Decks without stems simply get the normal EQ transition; nothing needs arming, and your own chip mutes always win.
  • Tune the cost — SETTINGS → Stems. Stems on up to caps how many decks may hold stems at once (Auto reads your installed RAM and names the number it chose). Separation speed — Gentle / Balanced / Fast — is how many cores an offline separation may use; it never touches playback. On Apple Silicon there is also an experimental CoreML acceleration toggle, off by default: where CoreML will not take the model Cadence separates on the CPU and says so, so it can never make a separation fail.
Memory, not magic. A stems deck keeps the whole track's four parts in RAM — around 210 MB for a 5-minute track — so the number of stems decks is capped, and asking for one past the cap is refused with a toast that names it. Change it under SETTINGS → Stems. Key-lock works with stems: a stemmed deck holds its pitch under SYNC exactly like any other.

Transport, sync & quantize

One quantize grid

The quantize setting (SETTINGS → Interface → Quantize: Off, 1/16 … 4 Bars) governs everything: clip launches and stops, seeks, beat-jumps, quantized mutes, and loop snapping — loop in/out points land on the chosen grid, so loop lengths come out quantized automatically. "Off" means immediate timing with beat-snapped loops.

Tempo

Typed or dragged tempo changes glide over several seconds rather than snapping (SYNC'd decks bend along smoothly). →BPM on a strip glides the master to that track's native tempo. Tap with T. Range 60–180 BPM.

Sync outputs

Cadence is a clock source: MIDI clock out (pick the port in the transport bar; free-run keeps clock flowing while stopped), Ableton Link for network tempo+phase, and OSC for visuals and modules (see OSC). Hosted plugins read the master playhead, so sequencer plugins run in lockstep.

Loops & loop rolls

Loops

  • Drag across a track overview to set a loop (edges snap to the quantize grid); drag the IN/OUT handles to resize — changes are adopted on the next beat, the music never jumps mid-cycle.
  • LOOP N on the strip loops N beats from the current beat; /2/x2 resize live, and the counter follows.
  • Saved loops (from the section editor or rekordbox import) appear dimmed in the overview and auto-engage when the playhead reaches them — prepare a "hold here" loop at the end of a track and it can't be missed.
  • If you drag a loop ahead of the playhead, the deck plays into it and then loops — it never yanks backwards.

Loop rolls

The 1/4 1/2 1 2 pads are momentary: hold to stutter a grid-aligned window while the real position keeps running underneath (watch the ghost playhead); release and the current repeat finishes on its boundary, then playback snaps back exactly in time. The pad stays lit until the release lands.

Key-lock & key matching

When SYNC bends a track's tempo, key-lock keeps its pitch constant — realtime, full-length tracks, all four decks at once (the engine has been measured at ~5% DSP with all four stretching at a 128-sample buffer).

  • KEY lit = key-lock on (default). Unlit = vinyl-style: pitch follows tempo.
  • Right-click KEY — shift the track's key by semitones (−6…+6), or Match CH n: Cadence reads both detected keys and applies the shortest shift to make them harmonically compatible (minor keys are compared through their relative major). The button shows the active shift ("KEY+2").
  • The key shift resets when a new track loads.
Harmonic browsing: in the COLLECTION, tracks whose key is compatible with the playing deck (same key, relative, or a wheel neighbour) tint green — pick your next track by colour. The Key column shows both the key name and the wheel code ("Am  8A").

Collection

Scanning

Scan analyses tracks (several in parallel): tempo and a bar-accurate beatgrid (comb-filtered detection with a kick-weighted downbeat vote), the musical key (tuning-corrected chromagram — works on A≠440 masters), and integrated loudness for auto-gain. The Grid column shows confidence; Verified means a human (or rekordbox) confirmed it.

Search

The search box filters as you type. Every space-separated term must match: words search title/genre/key, plain numbers match BPM ±1, and 120-126 filters a BPM range. Example: techno 7A 122-126. Esc clears.

Playlists & the right-click menu

Build playlists with New Playlist; multi-select tracks and right-click to add them. The right-click menu also offers: Load playing (fader down) (audition: the deck starts, channel on, fader at the bottom — ride it in by hand), Scan, Edit beatgrid…, Edit sections…, Edit track info…, Open in Editor, and removal options. The LOAD 1–4 buttons glow with each channel's live level so you never load over a playing deck.

Importing music

  • Add Files — WAV, AIFF, FLAC, MP3, OGG. Metadata tags (genre, key) are read when present.
  • Import XML — a rekordbox collection export (File → Export Collection in xml format). Brings in tracks, your curated beatgrids (marked Verified), up to four hot cues, saved loops, and the playlist tree. Existing tracks are updated, not duplicated; a summary reports anything missing on disk.

Imported grids are trusted as-is; keys and loudness still benefit from a Cadence scan.

Grid & section editors

Beatgrid editor

Grid opens the beatgrid editor: set the tempo and drag the downbeat by eye against the waveform. Applying marks the grid Verified and pushes it live to any loaded deck. The /2 and x2 buttons next to the BPM field fix octave-doubled or halved detections.

Section editor

Sections opens the section/cue/loop editor (it deliberately never touches the beatgrid). Add any number of coloured, labelled sections snapped to the grid — they show up in the collection previews, the mixer overviews and the Composer timeline, so you read a track's structure everywhere. Also set up to four hot cues and a saved loop here.

Composer

A DAW-style timeline for preparing a set. Drag tracks from the docked collection onto a lane — each becomes a full-length clip so you can see where it ends and line up the next one. When the master playhead crosses a clip, its deck launches.

  • Clips: move, trim either edge, split with the CUT razor. The deck's live position is mirrored on the timeline.
  • Future loops: with the LOOP tool, drag a region on a clip — when the song reaches it, the deck stays in that loop (it pulses azure while engaged; edits to an engaged loop apply live).
  • Automation: the VOL / FILT / DLY / VERB modes draw envelopes per lane — played back into the channel knobs during the set (value labels show dB etc.; FILT has a centre line; CLEAR wipes the selected parameter's lane).
  • Navigation: +/− zoom (or ⌘-scroll), NOW keeps the view following the playhead, lane headers carry a volume slider and a live meter, and each lane glows with its channel's level.

Sampler (samples & strips)

A non-destructive sample editor — sources are only read, never modified.

  • Open a track: press BROWSE (or C) to dock the Collection under the editor, then double-click a track — or drag one onto the waveform. Right-clicking a track anywhere in the COLLECTION → Open in Sampler works too.
  • Select: drag on the zoomable waveform; SNAP locks edges to the beatgrid; drag the edges to adjust. The readout shows seconds and beats. Wheel zooms, shift-wheel pans.
  • Save Sample — writes the region as a 24-bit WAV into ~/Music/Cadence Samples and adds it to the collection (drum hits, vocals, stabs…).
  • Monitor it: PLAY plays the selection through the Sampler's own preview voice — instantly, straight from memory, with no deck involved. With nothing selected it plays the whole loaded track, so a file you just dragged in can be heard right away. LOOP repeats the region (the wrap is a hard splice, so you hear exactly what the saved sample will sound like), and a white playhead tracks the voice across the waveform.
  • Where it goes, and how loud: CUE and MAIN are two independent switches, not a choice of one — cue is your headphones, main is the room, and both can be on at once (or neither). CUE is on and MAIN is off by default, so pressing PLAY can never surprise a dance floor. VOL is the preview's own level; nothing on the mixer moves when you change it. Routing and level are remembered between sessions.
  • It is not a deck: the preview has no EQ, no filter, no FX and no channel fader, and it cannot take a deck away from you mid-set. (It replaces the old Audition CH4 button, which loaded a rendered temp file onto a deck — sometimes one that was live on the master.)
  • The STRIP: Add to Strip collects regions in order — from any number of tracks — and Render Strip bounces them into one joint clip (44.1k/24-bit, equal-power crossfades at every join). Your quick re-edit/mashup workflow.
  • Where they land: saved samples and rendered strips go to ~/Music/Cadence/Sampler Out and into the collection, tagged Sampler Out; the docked Collection below picks them up straight away.

Plugins, chains & macros

Chains

Every channel hosts up to three plugins in series (AU/VST3 effects or instruments). Click the strip's FX chip:

  • Loaded slots get submenus — open editor, bypass, remove, pin to favorites.
  • Favorites (your pinned list, then most-used) and the full manufacturer-grouped list load into the first empty slot; the editor pops open ready to tweak, floating above Cadence even fullscreen.
  • Plugin manager / scan… opens the manager: scan for plugins, double-click favorites onto an exact channel + slot (CH and S1–S3 selectors).

Chains, plugin settings, bypass states and macro assignments all persist — quit and relaunch, and your rig rebuilds itself.

Macros

See Master section — M1–M8 bind to plugin parameters by touch-learn or menu, are MIDI-learnable, and are controllable from the remote. Every hosted plugin editor carries a header strip with TO MACRO (send the last-touched parameter to a macro), BYPASS and REMOVE.

Idle DSP: loaded chains process every block, like DAW tracks — an idling synth costs real CPU. Bypass or remove chains you're not using (the FX chip menu) to reclaim it. The engine readout shows the average load.

MIDI routing & VST jamming

MIDI flows through each chain like a DAW track: slot 1's MIDI output feeds slot 2, and so on. That means a sequencer plugin in slot 1 (Tonnetz, Metro 185) plays an instrument in slot 2 (Tincture) on the same channel — locked to the master clock via the host playhead.

  • Cross-channel: the FX menu's MIDI out → CH n routes a chain's MIDI to another channel — an empty deck becomes a pure MIDI-send device driving an instrument elsewhere.
  • Key-follow: SHLabs modules with a FOLLOW switch (Tonnetz) listen to Cadence's module bus and lock their key/scale to whatever track currently leads the mix — the reference hands over automatically as the next song takes over. VST jams stay in key with the set.
  • Hardware/performance MIDI (a keyboard) feeds every chain alongside the routed events.

Remote control

Cadence serves its own browser remote on your local network — phone or tablet, no app. Press H and scan the "remote control" QR (or type the address shown).

  • Master: play/stop, BPM, beat dots.
  • Per deck: track name, PLAY, LOOP, position bar, a big fader, FADE IN/OUT (gradual 8-beat ramps) and MIX 32 / MIX 8 — auto-transition into that deck from the current lead, slow or fast.
  • Macros: all eight, as sliders.
  • Everything is quantized — remote actions land on the next bar while the clock runs.

Requirements: same Wi-Fi network (no guest/isolated VLAN); allow Cadence in the macOS firewall if prompted. Toggle the remote on/off by clicking the engine readout. Prefer a dedicated OSC app? Cadence's OSC-in mapping works with TouchOSC and friends too.

OSC & the module bus

Main OSC out

The OSC panel points a configurable target (host/port) at visualizers or show control — SHLabs Phosphor locks its visuals to it. Messages include /cadence/bpm, /cadence/beat, /cadence/phase, /cadence/transport, per-channel and master levels, band energies, kick onsets, a section-intensity signal, and the live section's index/type and progress-through.

Stem bus

When a deck is playing separated stems, Cadence also broadcasts what the mix is made of: /cadence/stems/drums, /bass, /other and /cadence/stems/vocals (0..1 levels of the audible mix, taken after the per-stem gains and mutes, so killing the drums takes them out of the feed) plus /cadence/stems/drums/onset, a per-hit spike from the drum stem alone that no bassline can trigger. Several stem decks are combined by taking the loudest, not by adding. /cadence/stems/active (0/1) says whether any audible deck is on stems — it repeats about twice a second so a module can join mid-set; the levels are sent only while it is 1.

Module bus

A second, always-on local feed (127.0.0.1:9009) carries clock, transport and /cadence/key — the live set's current key, including any key shift. SHLabs modules hosted on the same machine (Tonnetz's FOLLOW) use it without any setup, and without touching your main OSC target. Toggle it in the engine-options menu (click the DSP readout).

OSC in

Incoming OSC can be mapped generically to controls — see the OSC panel.

MIDI-learn

Press MIDI MAP to arm mapping mode: click a control on screen, then move a MIDI control (or send OSC) to bind it. Right-click a control clears its binding, and Esc ends the session. Faders, EQs, filters, cue/on buttons, deck play, sync, the auto-ramp chips, the eight macros, the stem chips and every clip-grid slot are mappable, with bank support for small controllers. Mappings persist.

The full list of bindings, the input device picker and your saved map profiles live in SETTINGS → MIDI mapping….

Recording

REC records the master bus (post compressor and limiter) as a 24-bit WAV into ~/Music/Cadence Recordings. Press again to stop.

Troubleshooting

  • The remote page won't load — phone and Mac must be on the same Wi-Fi (guest networks often isolate clients); allow Cadence in System Settings → Network → Firewall. The QR always encodes the current working address.
  • High DSP while idle — loaded plugin chains run like DAW tracks. Bypass or remove unused plugins via the FX chip.
  • Loops/cues don't snap — the track has no beatgrid yet: scan it, or set the grid by eye in the beatgrid editor.
  • Wrong tempo (half/double) — use /2 or x2 next to the BPM field in the collection.
  • SYNC'd track sounds pitched — KEY (key-lock) is off on that deck; light it up.
  • Key column empty — keys come from scanning (or rekordbox import).
  • A deck won't launch — the master clock must be running; launches are quantized, so they wait for the grid point set by Quantize in SETTINGS.
  • Audio device vanished — the red banner means the interface disconnected; Cadence reconnects automatically when it returns.
  • No STEMS row on the deck — that track hasn't been separated yet: right-click it in the COLLECTION → Separate stems (and install the model in SETTINGS first, if you haven't).
  • Stems won't turn on — you are at the deck cap; turn them off on another deck, or raise SETTINGS → Stems → Stems on up to.
  • The mixer disappeared — the console header cycles full → levels → hidden. Click MIXER - HIDDEN at the top of the deck area to bring it back.
